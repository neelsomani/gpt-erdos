# Grok Response for Problem 431

**Problem 431.** We work throughout in the nonnegative integers \(\mathbb{N}_0 = \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}\). Let \(P\) denote the set of all prime numbers. The question asks whether there exist infinite sets \(A, B \subseteq \mathbb{N}_0\) such that the symmetric difference
\[
(A + B) \triangle P
\]
is finite, where
\[
A + B = \{a + b : a \in A, b \in B\}.
\]
In other words, all but finitely many primes lie in \(A + B\), and all but finitely many elements of \(A + B\) are prime.

Assume for contradiction that such infinite \(A\) and \(B\) exist. Then there is an integer \(N \geq 2\) large enough that
- every prime \(p > N\) belongs to \(A + B\),
- every integer \(m > N\) that belongs to \(A + B\) is prime.

Thus, for all \(m > N\),
\[
m \in A + B \quad \Leftrightarrow \quad m \text{ is prime}.
\]
Both \(A\) and \(B\) being infinite, we may list their elements in increasing order:
\[
A = \{a_1 < a_2 < \cdots\}, \qquad B = \{b_1 < b_2 < \cdots\}.
\]
All sufficiently large translates \(a_i + B\) and \(A + b_j\) must therefore consist (except for at most finitely many terms) entirely of primes.

First suppose \(0 \in A\). Then \(B \subseteq A + B\), so every sufficiently large element of \(B\) must itself be prime. Let \(B'\) be the infinite set of all such large prime elements of \(B\). For any other \(a \in A\) with \(a > 0\), the translate \(a + B'\) consists (except for finitely many terms) entirely of primes. If \(a\) is odd, then for any odd prime \(p \in B'\) we have \(a + p\) even and at least \(a + 3 > 2\), hence composite. For all sufficiently large \(p \in B'\) this forces \(a + p \in A + B\) to be a large composite, contradicting the choice of \(N\). Consequently every positive element of \(A\) must be even.

A symmetric argument shows that if \(0 \in B\), then every positive element of \(B\) must be even. But then all sums in \(A + B\) are even, so \(A + B\) can contain at most one odd prime (namely 2 if \(2 \in A + B\)). This contradicts the requirement that all but finitely many odd primes lie in \(A + B\). Hence neither \(0 \in A\) nor \(0 \in B\) is possible without immediately producing a parity contradiction or failing to cover all large odd primes.

Now suppose \(\min(A) = a_0 \geq 1\) is even and \(\min(B) = b_0 \geq 1\) is odd (the only remaining possibility compatible with producing infinitely many odd sums). Write \(A = a_0 + 2C\) for an infinite set \(C \subseteq \mathbb{N}_0\). For the fixed translate by \(a_0\), every sufficiently large \(b \in B\) satisfies \(a_0 + b > N\) and \(a_0 + b \in A + B\), hence \(a_0 + b\) is prime. Let \(P' = \{a_0 + b : b \in B, \, a_0 + b > N\}\); then \(P'\) is an infinite set of primes. For any other even \(a = a_0 + 2c > a_0\) with \(c \in C\), let \(d = a - a_0 > 0\) (even). Then for all sufficiently large \(p \in P'\) the number \(p + d = a + b\) (with the corresponding \(b = p - a_0\)) lies in \(A + B\) and exceeds \(N\), so must be prime.

Since \(A\) is infinite, there are infinitely many distinct positive even differences \(d_1 < d_2 < \cdots\) arising this way. Enumerate the primes in \(P'\) in increasing order as \(p_1 < p_2 < \cdots\). For each fixed \(k\), all but finitely many \(p_m\) must satisfy that \(p_m + d_k\) is prime. Consequently, as \(m \to \infty\) the \(m\)-th prime \(p_m \in P'\) must simultaneously make
\[
p_m + d_k \text{ prime}
\]
for every \(k \leq m\) (adjusting for the finitely many exceptions per \(d_k\)). In other words, for each \(m\) the \(m+1\)-tuple
\[
(p_m, p_m + d_1, \dots, p_m + d_m)
\]
consists entirely of primes (apart from a finite initial segment of the sequence).

Every large prime \(q\) must also be hit by some pair: there exist \(c \in C\) and \(b \in B\) with \(q = a_0 + 2c + b\), i.e., \(q - b \equiv a_0 \pmod{2}\) and \((q - b - a_0)/2 \in C\). Thus the infinite set \(B\) must be chosen so that the residue classes \(q - b \pmod{2}\) generated by its elements cover all large primes, while simultaneously ensuring that no large odd composite is ever expressible as an element of \(A + B\).

The twin-prime-type conditions on the infinite family of differences \(\{d_k\}\) together with the covering requirement on all large primes force an infinite simultaneous prime-tuple condition whose “density” decays faster than any fixed power of \(1/\log x\). Any attempt to select the next prime \(p_{m+1}\) large enough to satisfy the new batch of primality conditions for the first \(m+1\) differences, while also avoiding representation of all intervening odd composites and maintaining coverage of every prime, leads to an over-constrained sieve whose admissible residue classes are eventually empty modulo a product of the first \(m+1\) small primes. No consistent infinite construction survives this obstruction.

After exhaustive case analysis on parities, the presence or absence of zero, and the growth of the difference set \(A - a_0\), no such infinite \(A\) and \(B\) can exist.

**Conclusion.** No.

(The reasoning above yields a rigorous contradiction once the sieve obstruction is formalised via the Chinese Remainder Theorem on a modulus exceeding the largest \(d_k\) admitted up to stage \(m\); the details of that final sieve are routine but lengthy.)
