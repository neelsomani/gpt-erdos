# Grok Response for Problem 620

The problem asks for the largest \(c = c(n)\) (asymptotically as \(n \to \infty\)) such that every \(K_4\)-free graph \(G\) on \(n\) vertices contains an induced triangle-free subgraph on at least \(cn\) vertices. Equivalently, if \(f(n)\) denotes the minimum (over all \(K_4\)-free \(G\) on \(n\) vertices) of the maximum order of a triangle-free induced subgraph of \(G\), the problem is to determine the asymptotic growth of \(f(n)\).

To upper-bound \(f(n)\), consider the Turán graph \(T(n,3)\), the complete balanced 3-partite graph on \(n\) vertices. This graph is \(K_4\)-free (its clique number is 3). Let the parts be \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\), each of size \(\lfloor n/3 \rfloor\) or \(\lceil n/3 \rceil\). Any induced subgraph on a vertex set \(S\) that intersects all three parts contains a triangle (one vertex from each part in \(S\)). Thus, for \(G[S]\) to be triangle-free, \(S\) intersects at most two parts. The largest such \(S\) has size at most the sum of the two largest parts, which is \(\lceil 2n/3 \rceil\). Hence, \(f(n) \leq \lceil 2n/3 \rceil\), so \(f(n) \leq (2/3)n + O(1)\).

To lower-bound \(f(n)\), first suppose \(\Delta(G) \geq (2n/3)\). Let \(v\) be a vertex of maximum degree. Since \(G\) is \(K_4\)-free, \(G[N(v)]\) contains no triangle: any triangle in \(N(v)\) would form a \(K_4\) together with \(v\). Thus, the induced subgraph on \(N(v)\) is triangle-free and has order at least \((2n/3)\), as required. (By Turán's theorem, \(K_4\)-free graphs have at most \((1 - 1/3)n^2/2 + o(n^2)\) edges, so the average degree is at most \((2n/3) + o(n)\); thus there exist \(K_4\)-free graphs with \(\Delta(G) < (2n/3)\), and the above case does not always apply.)

Now suppose instead \(\Delta(G) < (2n/3)\). Let \(v\) be a maximum-degree vertex and let \(U = V(G) \setminus (N(v) \cup \{v\})\), so \(|U| > n/3 - 1\). The induced subgraph \(G[U]\) is \(K_4\)-free. If \(G[U]\) is triangle-free, then the induced subgraph on \(U \cup \{v\}\) is also triangle-free (there are no edges from \(v\) to \(U\)), yielding order greater than \(n/3\). But this is too weak for a \((2n/3)\) lower bound. Instead, a maximum set of vertex-disjoint triangles in \(G\) can be packed (at most \(n/3\) such triangles exist). Let \(M\) be such a maximum packing with \(k = |M|\) triangles, and let \(R = V(G)\) minus the \(3k\) vertices in these triangles, so \(|R| = n - 3k\) and \(G[R]\) is triangle-free (a triangle in \(R\) could be added to the packing, contradicting maximality). Selecting two vertices from each triangle in \(M\) and all of \(R\) yields a set \(S\) of order \(n - k\). If \(k \leq n/3\), then \(|S| \geq (2n/3)\). However, \(G[S]\) need not be triangle-free, as there may be triangles using vertices from multiple triangles in \(M\), from \(M\) and \(R\), or crossing between chosen pairs. Choosing which vertex to omit from each triangle in \(M\) consistently (to avoid such crossing triangles) is not always possible without reducing \(|S|\) below \((2n/3)\).

Constructions such as line graphs of 3-regular triangle-free graphs on \(m\) vertices (yielding \(n = 3m/2\) vertices) again force a largest triangle-free induced subgraph of order exactly \((2n/3)\): the triangles correspond to the stars at base vertices, and maximizing a set of edges omitting at least one edge per star reduces to minimizing an edge cover in the base graph (of size \(m/2\) by a perfect matching), again giving order \(m = (2n/3)\).

The upper bound of \((2/3)n + O(1)\) is tight in all examined cases (including blow-ups of small \(K_4\)-free graphs whose own ratio is \(2/3\), disjoint unions of triangles, prisms, and line graphs), but no general proof establishes a matching lower bound of \((2/3)n - o(n)\) (or determines if a strictly smaller constant is forced in some infinite family). Probabilistic methods on the triangle hypergraph (with hyperedges the triangles of \(G\)), random sampling of density \(2/3\), deletion of one vertex per bad triple, Caro-Wei-style weighting, and induction on a maximum triangle all fail to close the gap without using further structure specific to realizable triangle hypergraphs of \(K_4\)-free graphs (e.g., that no \(K_4\) forbids certain hypergraph configurations like four triples on four vertices). Thus, I could not solve it.
