# Grok Response for Problem 740

The problem asks whether every graph \(G\) with \(\chi(G)=\mathfrak{m}\) (for infinite cardinal \(\mathfrak{m}\)) must contain a subgraph \(H\) with \(\chi(H)=\mathfrak{m}\) such that \(H\) contains no odd cycle of length at most \(r\).

To approach this, first note that \(\chi(G)=\mathfrak{m}\) means that \(G\) admits no proper vertex-coloring with fewer than \(\mathfrak{m}\) colors. Equivalently, in any partition of \(V(G)\) into fewer than \(\mathfrak{m}\) parts, at least one part contains an edge of \(G\). A subgraph \(H\subseteq G\) (on the same vertex set, or possibly a subgraph induced on a subset) satisfies \(\chi(H)=\mathfrak{m}\) if the same holds with edges restricted to \(E(H)\). The additional condition requires that \(H\) contains no subgraph isomorphic to \(C_{2k+1}\) for any odd length \(2k+1\leq r\); in other words, every odd cycle in \(H\) (if any) has length at least \(r+2\).

One direction is immediate: there exist graphs of chromatic number \(\mathfrak{m}\) with no odd cycles of length at most \(r\) at all (for any fixed \(r\)). To see this without relying on external constructions, begin with the complete graph \(K_{\mathfrak{m}}\), which has \(\chi(K_{\mathfrak{m}})=\mathfrak{m}\). Any graph on at most \(\mathfrak{m}\) vertices arises as a subgraph of \(K_{\mathfrak{m}}\) by appropriate edge selection. It is therefore enough to produce (on a vertex set of size \(\mathfrak{m}\)) a graph \(H_0\) with \(\chi(H_0)=\mathfrak{m}\) and no short odd cycles, as this \(H_0\) embeds into \(K_{\mathfrak{m}}\) and hence serves as a witness in that special case. To build such an \(H_0\), proceed by transfinite recursion on \(\mathfrak{m}\). Enumerate the vertices as \(\{v_\alpha:\alpha<\mathfrak{m}\}\). At stage \(\alpha\), the preceding vertices \(\{v_\beta:\beta<\alpha\}\) span a graph \(H_\alpha\) of size \(|\alpha|<\mathfrak{m}\). Ensure \(\chi(H_\alpha)<\mathfrak{m}\) (possible since fewer than \(\mathfrak{m}\) vertices are available so far) while maintaining the no-short-odd-cycle condition up to this stage. When adding \(v_\alpha\), connect it to a subset \(S\subseteq\{v_\beta:\beta<\alpha\}\) chosen so that:
- No new odd cycle of length \(\leq r\) is created (finitely many forbidden configurations per potential neighbor, and \(|S|<\mathfrak{m}\), so avoidance is possible by omitting at most \(<\mathfrak{m}\) choices per forbidden pattern).
- The addition increases the "chromatic pressure": specifically, ensure that for every \(\lambda<\mathfrak{m}\), there are finite subsets on which the induced chromatic number eventually exceeds any prescribed finite bound (to force overall \(\chi=\mathfrak{m}\)). This can be arranged by maintaining, at each stage, a collection of \(\mathfrak{m}\)-many finite "witness" sets that are not \(\lambda\)-colorable in the partial graph for all \(\lambda<\mathfrak{m}\), and ensuring the new vertex completes a non-\(\lambda\)-colorable configuration in at least one such witness per \(\lambda\).

The recursion succeeds because at each \(\alpha<\mathfrak{m}\) only \(<\mathfrak{m}\) many constraints are active (finitely many per forbidden cycle pattern, and \(\mathfrak{m}\) many color-witness conditions, but each witnessed on a finite set). The final \(H_0\) on \(\mathfrak{m}\) vertices then has no odd cycle of length \(\leq r\) (all potential short cycles are checked at finite stages) and \(\chi(H_0)=\mathfrak{m}\) (every coloring with \(\lambda<\mathfrak{m}\) colors fails on one of the maintained witnesses). Thus such graphs exist, so the complete graph \(K_{\mathfrak{m}}\) contains the desired subgraph.

For a general \(G\) with \(\chi(G)=\mathfrak{m}\), however, the situation is more delicate. The above recursion cannot be carried out inside an arbitrary \(G\), because the available edges at each stage are restricted to those already present in \(G\). One might attempt to select a subset of edges from \(G\) directly (i.e., build \(H\) by including only non-forbidden edges from \(G\)) while preserving the chromatic pressure. Suppose we try to maintain, by transfinite recursion on ordinals \(<\mathfrak{m}\), a partial edge set \(E_\alpha\subseteq E(G)\) on vertices enumerated up to \(\alpha\), ensuring:
- No odd cycle of length \(\leq r\) appears in \((V_\alpha,E_\alpha)\).
- For every \(\lambda<\mathfrak{m}\), there is a collection of \(\mathfrak{m}\)-many finite "obstruction sets" (each requiring more than \(\lambda\) colors) that remain non-\(\lambda\)-colorable in the partial \(H_\alpha\).

At successor stages, when adding a vertex or an edge from \(G\), the no-short-odd-cycle condition removes only finitely many candidate edges per vertex (those completing a forbidden cycle through already-decided edges). The difficulty is that the edges needed to "complete" an obstruction set to force high chromatic number may have been among those removed to avoid short odd cycles. There is no obvious way to prioritize the chromatic obstructions over the cycle-avoidance constraints (or vice versa) while guaranteeing that \(\mathfrak{m}\)-many obstructions survive at the end. If the only high-chromatic-pressure substructures in \(G\) rely critically on short odd cycles, the removal process could collapse \(\chi\) below \(\mathfrak{m}\).

An alternative attempt via compactness fails similarly. Consider the first-order language with a binary edge relation and \(\mathfrak{m}\)-many unary predicates (one per color class). The theory asserting "the edge relation is a subgraph of \(G\)", "no odd cycle of length \(\leq r\)" (finitely many forbidden configurations), and "the coloring predicates do not yield a proper \(\lambda\)-coloring for any \(\lambda<\mathfrak{m}\)" is finitely consistent (by the existence result above, or by taking finite high-chromatic subgraphs of \(G\) and deleting edges to break short odd cycles while retaining high chromatic number on those finite pieces). But compactness only yields models of size at most \(2^{<\mathfrak{m}}\) or so, and does not guarantee a model whose edge relation is exactly a subgraph of the given \(G\) (the compactness is in the abstract language, not anchored inside \(G\)).

No obvious reduction to the finite case works either. While \(\chi(G)=\mathfrak{m}\) implies (by repeated application of the finite de Bruijn–Erdős theorem) that \(G\) contains finite subgraphs of arbitrarily high chromatic number, extracting a single infinite subgraph \(H\subseteq G\) that is simultaneously free of all short odd cycles *and* contains finite subgraphs of arbitrarily high chromatic number requires uniform control over the cycle deletions across all those finite pieces. Aligning the edge deletions so that no short odd cycle appears globally may destroy the chromatic obstructions in some of the finite pieces.

After considering these approaches (recursion inside \(G\), compactness in an expanded language, and reduction to finite high-chromatic subgraphs), none yields a definite yes or no answer. Thus, I could not solve the problem.
