# Grok Response for Problem 768

The set \(A\) consists of those \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) (including \(n=1\), vacuously) such that if \(p \mid n\) is prime, then there is a divisor \(d \mid n\) with \(d > 1\) and \(p \mid (d-1)\). Equivalently, if \(m_p = n/p^{v_p(n)}\) denotes the \(p\)-free part of \(n\), then the divisors of \(m_p\) greater than 1 must include at least one in the residue class \(1 \pmod{p}\).

First, \(A\) is contained in the set of \(n \leq N\) with largest prime factor \(P(n) \leq \sqrt{N}\). To see this, let \(P = P(n)\) and write \(n = P^k \cdot m\) with \(P \nmid m\). Any divisor \(d \mid n\) with \(P \nmid d\) necessarily satisfies \(d \mid m\), so \(d \leq m \leq N/P\). If \(P > \sqrt{N}\), then \(m < P\), whence \(d < P\). But any \(d > 1\) with \(d \equiv 1 \pmod{P}\) must satisfy \(d \geq P+1 > P\), a contradiction. Thus no such \(d\) exists for this \(P\), so \(n \notin A\). (This already shows that no primes, prime powers, or semiprimes lie in \(A\).)

This necessary condition alone does not force \(|A \cap [1,N]|/N \to 0\), since the count of \(\sqrt{N}\)-smooth integers up to \(N\) is \(\Psi(N,\sqrt{N}) \sim \rho(2)N\) with Dickman-de Bruijn function value \(\rho(2) = 1 - \log 2 \approx 0.3069 > 0\).

However, the condition is substantially stricter. For a prime \(p \mid n\), the existence of \(d > 1\) dividing the \(p\)-free part with \(d \equiv 1 \pmod{p}\) requires that \(p\) divides \(d-1\) for some such \(d \geq p+1\). Thus \(n\) must be divisible by both \(p\) and (the prime factors of) this \(d\). If \(p\) is a large prime factor of \(n\) (close to \(\sqrt{N}\)), then \(d \gtrsim p\) forces the \(p\)-free part of \(n\) to be at least size \(\approx p\), so \(n \gtrsim p^2\). This is possible (e.g., if \(p\) divides \(2^k-1\) for Mersenne primes \(p\), taking \(d = 2^k\) with \(k\) large enough that \(2^k \mid n\), or more generally if \(p \mid (d-1)\) for composite smooth \(d > 1\)), but such \(p\) must then satisfy \(p \lesssim \sqrt{N}\). Moreover, the prime factors of \(d\) must themselves satisfy the condition for \(A\) (now with respect to all primes dividing \(n\)).

Examples show this is possible with one large prime: e.g., \(n=56=2^3 \cdot 7\) lies in \(A\) since \(7 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}\) and \(8 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}\). But Mersenne primes (or primes dividing \(d-1\) for small smooth \(d\)) are sparse, contributing \(o(N^\epsilon)\) to the count up to \(N\) for any \(\epsilon > 0\). Thus they do not affect the asymptotic density.

The main contribution to \(|A \cap [1,N]|\) must therefore arise from integers \(n \leq N\) whose prime factors are all small (i.e., \(y\)-smooth for \(y = o(\sqrt{N})\)), but with the full set of prime factors satisfying the covering condition simultaneously: for each such \(p\), the semigroup of divisors generated multiplicatively by the other prime powers in \(n\) (modulo \(p\)) must hit the residue \(1 \pmod{p}\) nontrivially. When all prime factors are \(\leq y\) with \(y\) small and \(n\) has sufficiently high multiplicities, the divisors modulo \(p\) (with \(p \leq y\)) densely populate \((\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*\) or its subgroups, making the condition easier to satisfy. (If exponents exceed the orders of the generators in \((\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*\), the full subgroup generated by the other primes is typically hit, and nontrivial relations to \(1\) become likely for small fixed \(p\).)

To obtain the precise order, suppose the dominant \(n \in A\) are those that are \(y\)-smooth up to \(N\) for a variable \(y = \exp(c \sqrt{\log N})\) (to be optimized over \(c > 0\)). Then \(u = \log N / \log y \sim \sqrt{\log N}/c\), and
\[
\frac{\Psi(N,y)}{N} \asymp \rho(u),
\]
where \(\rho(u)\) is the Dickman-de Bruijn function (\(\rho(u) = 1\) for \(0 \leq u \leq 1\), and \(u\rho'(u) = -\rho(u-1)\) for \(u > 1\)). For large \(u\), the asymptotic
\[
\log \rho(u) = -u(\log u + \log\log u - 1 + o(1))
\]
holds. Substituting \(u \sim \sqrt{\log N}/c\) yields
\[
\log \rho(u) \sim -\frac{\sqrt{\log N}}{c} \Bigl(\tfrac12\log\log N + O(\log\log\log N)\Bigr) = -\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2c} + o(1)\Bigr)\sqrt{\log N} \cdot \log\log N.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{\Psi(N,y)}{N} = \exp\bigl(-(c' + o(1))\sqrt{\log N} \cdot \log\log N\bigr)
\]
for \(c' = 1/(2c)\). If almost all such \(y\)-smooth \(n\) (with this choice of \(y\)) lie in \(A\) (or differ in count by a factor \(1+o(1)\)), or if the optimal \(y\) (balancing the smoothness constraint against the covering condition over all primes \(\leq y\)) occurs at some fixed \(c > 0\), then the claimed form holds for some \(c > 0\).

The examples, necessary smoothness constraints, and matching form of the Dickman asymptotic for \(u \sim \sqrt{\log N}\) make this plausible, but a rigorous proof requires showing that the covering condition holds with high probability in this smoothness range (or fails with negligible probability outside it), along with a precise optimization over the cutoff \(y\). This appears difficult and is left unresolved.
