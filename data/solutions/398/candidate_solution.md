Your equation
[
n!=x^2-1
]
is equivalent to
[
n!+1=x^2,
]
i.e. “when is $n!+1$ a perfect square?” This is the **Brocard–Ramanujan equation** (often called **Brocard’s problem**). ([Wikipedia][1])

### Known integer solutions

The only **known** values of $n$ giving an integer $x$ are
[
4!+1=25=5^2,\qquad 5!+1=121=11^2,\qquad 7!+1=5041=71^2,
]
so in terms of $(n,x)$ the known solutions are
[
(n,x)=(4,\pm 5),\ (5,\pm 11),\ (7,\pm 71).
]
([Wikipedia][1])

### Are these the *only* solutions?

No proof is known. It is **conjectured** that there are no other positive-integer solutions beyond (n=4,5,7), but this remains **open**. ([Wikipedia][1])

What *is* known is that extensive computations have found no further solutions in large ranges. For example, a standard cited result is that there are no other solutions with (n<10^9). 

So: **the only solutions currently known are at (n=4,5,7), but it is not known (proved) that these are the only ones.**
