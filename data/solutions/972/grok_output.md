The reformulation |α − q/p| < 1/p² with both p and q prime follows at once from the definition q = ⌊p α⌋ (the length of the interval (q/α, (q + 1)/α) is exactly 1/α < 1, so it contains at most one integer, which must be the candidate p). Equivalently, one asks whether the orbit of primes q under multiplication by the irrational 1/α produces infinitely many values whose distance to the nearest integer is < 1/α and for which that nearest integer is itself prime. Because {q/α} (mod 1) is dense in [0, 1] for prime q (by Vinogradov’s equidistribution theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions combined with Weyl’s criterion), a positive proportion ≈ 1/α of all primes q yield an integer candidate p ≈ q/α. Heuristically the primality of this p is independent of the primality of q for large q, the probability that p is prime is ∼ 1/ln(q/α) ∼ 1/ln q, and the prime-sum ∑ 1/ln q diverges (by the integral test: ∫₂^X dt/(ln t)² ∼ X/(ln X)² → ∞). This suggests infinitely many successes, yet turning the divergence into a rigorous proof requires controlling the simultaneous primality of two integers lying at irrational distance ≈ α from each other, a Diophantine condition not known to be compatible with any sieve of level sufficient to detect primes. Standard sieve methods (Selberg, Brun, or combinatorial sieves) lose too many terms when the sieve support must vary with the fractional part {q/α}, while results on primes in short intervals do not apply because the admissible interval length is bounded by the constant 1/α < 1. Known theorems on linear forms in primes (e.g., Maynard–Tao) likewise give no leverage, as the coefficient α is irrational and the admissible tuples cannot be placed in a fixed admissible set of linear forms. The same obstruction persists for every fixed irrational α > 1; no special continued-fraction or Diophantine-property assumption on α appears to circumvent it. Consequently the existence question remains open.
