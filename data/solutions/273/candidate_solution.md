If you mean a **distinct** covering system (all moduli different), then this is **still open**: nobody knows whether such a covering system exists, and nobody has proved it cannot exist. It is listed as an Erdős–Graham problem (Erdős Problem #273). ([Erdős Problems][1])

A small extra fact that is known: **if you also allow (p=3)** (so modulus (2=p-1) is allowed), then Selfridge found an example using moduli among the divisors of (360). ([Erdős Problems][1])

(If repeated moduli were allowed, it would be trivial: you could take all four residue classes mod (4=5-1). But that is not the interesting “covering system” question Erdős and Graham meant.)
