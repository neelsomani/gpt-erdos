The condition requires a set \(A \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}\) equipped with signs \(\delta_n = \pm 1\) such that the only subsets of the signed reciprocals \(\{\delta_n / n : n \in A\}\) summing to zero are the empty set and the full set.

To establish a lower bound on the maximum possible \(|A|\), consider sets containing 1 with all other signs negative. Let \(B \subseteq \{2, \dots, N\}\) satisfy \(\sum_{b \in B} 1/b = 1\). Set \(A = \{1\} \cup B\) with \(\delta_1 = +1\) and \(\delta_b = -1\) for all \(b \in B\). The full signed sum is then \(+1 - \sum_{b \in B} 1/b = 0\).

To verify the subset condition, clear a common denominator \(D = \operatorname{lcm}(A)\). This yields positive integers \(v_1 = D/1 = D\) and \(v_b = D/b\) (\(b \in B\)) with \(\sum_{b \in B} v_b = D = v_1\). Any nonempty subset sum of the signed terms (with these fixed signs) is zero only if it equals zero after scaling by \(D > 0\). Subsets not containing the term for 1 yield a nonpositive sum (strictly negative if nonempty). Subsets containing the term for 1 but only a proper subcollection of terms from \(B\) yield \(D - s > 0\), where \(s < D\) is the subsum of the corresponding \(v_b > 0\). The only way to obtain zero is thus to include all terms (the full set). Hence any such \(A\) is valid, and the maximum \(|A|\) is at least one more than the maximum cardinality of a set \(B \subseteq \{2, \dots, N\}\) with reciprocal sum exactly 1.

Symmetrically, one may take any \(m \in \{1, \dots, N\}\) in place of 1 (as the unique positive term) provided there exists \(B \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\} \setminus \{m\}\) with \(\sum_{b \in B} 1/b = 1/m\) and all elements of \(B\) distinct from \(m\); the same one-positive argument applies after clearing denominators (all negative absolute values are positive integers strictly less than the cleared value for \(m\), so no proper subsum of negatives can cancel it exactly). This yields the same lower bound construction, optimized over the choice of \(m\).

To upper-bound the maximum \(|A|\), suppose \(|A| = k\) with signs \(\delta\) as in the problem. There are \(2^k - 2\) nonempty proper subsets whose signed reciprocal sums must avoid zero. Clearing a common denominator \(D = \operatorname{lcm}(A)\) reduces to an equivalent problem on integers \(v_n = D/n\) (\(n \in A\)): there is a choice of signs such that the only subset of \(\{\delta_n v_n\}\) summing to zero is the full collection. Since the \(v_n\) are positive integers at most \(D\), the possible subset sums lie in \([-D \cdot H_N, D \cdot H_N]\) (where \(H_N\) is the \(N\)th harmonic number). However, this interval length alone does not tightly constrain \(k\), as subset sums need not be distinct (only nonzero where required). Linear dependence over \(\mathbb{Q}\) is guaranteed for \(k \geq 2\), but the restriction to coefficients in \(\{-1, 0, +1\}\) (with exactly the all-\(\pm 1\) case yielding zero) does not immediately cap \(k\) below \(\Theta(N)\), consistent with the construction above.
