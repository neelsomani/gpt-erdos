# Grok Response for Problem 151

The quantity \(\tau(G)\) is the transversal number of the hypergraph \(\mathcal{H}\) whose vertex set is \(V(G)\) and whose hyperedges are precisely the maximal cliques of \(G\) of cardinality at least 2. Equivalently, \(\tau(G)\) is the smallest size of a set \(S \subseteq V(G)\) such that \(V(G) \setminus S\) contains no hyperedge of \(\mathcal{H}\) as a subset.

Let \(F\) be the graph on vertex set \(V(G)\) whose edges are exactly those pairs \(\{u, v\}\) that form a maximal clique of \(G\). Then \(F\) is triangle-free: if \(F\) contained a triangle on vertices \(a, b, c\), the three edges of this triangle in \(F\) would be maximal cliques of \(G\), but these three vertices would induce a \(K_3\) in \(G\), contradicting maximality of the \(K_2\)'s. By definition of \(H(n)\), every triangle-free graph on \(n\) vertices (in particular \(F\)) has independence number at least \(H(n)\).

The hyperedges of \(\mathcal{H}\) consist of the edges of \(F\) together with a (possibly empty) collection \(\mathcal{L}\) of additional hyperedges, each of cardinality at least 3. Each member of \(\mathcal{L}\) is an independent set in \(F\): if \(\ell \in \mathcal{L}\) contained an edge of \(F\), that edge would be a maximal \(K_2\) of \(G\), but \(\ell\) is a clique of cardinality at least 3 containing this edge, again contradicting maximality. Thus \(\tau(G)\) equals the minimum cardinality of a set \(S \subseteq V(G)\) that is a vertex cover of \(F\) and that intersects every member of \(\mathcal{L}\). Equivalently, there must exist an independent set \(I\) of \(F\) with \(|I| \geq H(n)\) such that no member of \(\mathcal{L}\) is contained in \(I\); in this case \(S = V(G) \setminus I\) is as required.

When \(\mathcal{L} = \emptyset\) (i.e., when \(G\) is triangle-free), every independent set of \(F = G\) avoids containing a member of \(\mathcal{L}\), so the existence of such an \(I\) is immediate from the definition of \(H(n)\) and we have equality \(\tau(G) = n - \alpha(G) \leq n - H(n)\).

In general, however, it is necessary to find an independent set of \(F\) of size at least \(H(n)\) that additionally avoids containing any member of \(\mathcal{L}\) as a subset. While every member of \(\mathcal{L}\) is itself an independent set in \(F\), the maximality condition on these cliques in \(G\) imposes that no vertex of \(V(G) \setminus \ell\) (for \(\ell \in \mathcal{L}\)) is adjacent in \(G\) to all of \(\ell\). The edges of \(G\) are exactly the edges of \(F\) together with all pairs internal to some member of \(\mathcal{L}\). This additional structure on the members of \(\mathcal{L}\) (as maximal cliques) constrains which independent sets of \(F\) can contain them, but it is unclear how to exploit this to produce the desired independent set of \(F\) in all cases (for instance, via replacement of one independent set by another of the same cardinality that avoids a given \(\ell \in \mathcal{L}\), or via a direct Ramsey-theoretic argument on an auxiliary hypergraph).
