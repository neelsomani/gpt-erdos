(If some \(\alpha(G_i) \geq r+1\), an independent set of size \(r+1\) in \(G_i\) induces a copy of \(K_{r+1}\) with no edges of color \(i\).) Equivalently, every subset of \(r+1\) vertices must span at least one edge in each \(G_i\).

This justifies the hypothesis \(r \geq 3\).

The \(r^2\) edges incident to \(v\) are colored with \(r\) colors, so the degrees \(d_1(v), \dots, d_r(v)\) in the color classes satisfy \(\sum d_i(v) = r^2\). By the pigeonhole principle, \(\max_i d_i(v) \geq r\). Without loss of generality, assume \(d_1(v) \geq r\) and let \(U\) be the set of neighbors of \(v\) joined by color-1 edges, so \(|U| = d_1(v) \geq r\).

For this induced \(K_{r+1}\) to use all \(r\) colors (as required in a counterexample), the edges within \(T\) must include all colors from \(\{2, \dots, r\}\) at least once. In other words, in the induced edge-coloring on every such \(T\), none of the colors \(2, \dots, r\) can be absent.

If there are no color-1 edges within \(U\), then the induced subgraph on \(U\) is edge-colored with only the \(r-1\) colors \(\{2, \dots, r\}\).
