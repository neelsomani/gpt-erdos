# Grok Response for Problem 558

**The multicolor Ramsey number \(R(K_{s,t};k)\)**

The function \(R(G;k)\) is the smallest integer \(m\) such that every \(k\)-edge-coloring of \(K_m\) contains a monochromatic copy of \(G\). Here \(G = K_{s,t}\) (with \(s,t \geq 1\) and, without loss of generality, \(s \leq t\)).

First consider the case \(k=1\). A single color means the entire graph is monochromatic, and \(K_m\) contains a (not necessarily induced) subgraph isomorphic to \(K_{s,t}\) if and only if \(m \geq s+t\), since \(K_{s,t}\) has exactly \(s+t\) vertices. Thus \(R(K_{s,t};1) = s+t\).

Next consider the case \(s=1\) (so \(G = K_{1,t}\) is a star with \(t\) leaves). A monochromatic copy is equivalent to some vertex having monochromatic degree at least \(t\). In \(K_m\) each vertex has degree \(m-1\). If \(m-1 \leq k(t-1)\), it is possible to color the edges incident to any fixed vertex so that its degree in each color is at most \(t-1\); extending consistently over all vertices yields a \(k\)-coloring of \(K_{k(t-1)+1}\) with no monochromatic \(K_{1,t}\). For \(m = k(t-1)+2\) we have \(m-1 = k(t-1)+1\), so by the pigeonhole principle at least one color appears on at least \(t\) edges incident to any given vertex. Hence \(R(K_{1,t};k) = k(t-1)+2\). (The case \(t=1\) is symmetric, and when \(s=t=1\) we recover \(R(K_{1,1};k)=2\), as expected since \(K_{1,1}\) is a single edge.)

For \(s,t \geq 2\) the situation is more subtle. A monochromatic \(K_{s,t}\) in color \(c\) consists of disjoint sets \(A,B\) (\(|A|=s\), \(|B|=t\)) such that every edge between \(A\) and \(B\) receives color \(c\). Equivalently, the \(s\) vertices of \(A\) have \(t\) common neighbors in color \(c\) (or symmetrically for \(B\)). Thus the extremal question is the largest \(m\) admitting a \(k\)-edge-coloring in which every set of \(s\) vertices has at most \(t-1\) common neighbors in any single color (and symmetrically).

The Kővári–Sós–Turán theorem supplies an upper bound on the number of edges in a \(K_{s,t}\)-free graph on \(m\) vertices:
\[
\operatorname{ex}(m,K_{s,t}) \leq \frac12(s-1)^{1/t}m^{2-1/t} + \frac12(t-1)m.
\]
If \(k \cdot \operatorname{ex}(m,K_{s,t}) < \binom{m}{2}\), no such edge-partition exists and a monochromatic \(K_{s,t}\) must appear. For fixed \(s,t,k\) this forces \(R(K_{s,t};k) = O(k^{s})\) (assuming \(s \leq t\)), but the constant and exact threshold are not recovered. The bound is tight enough to prove finiteness but too coarse for an exact closed-form expression.

Explicit constructions for lower bounds can be obtained in special cases by decomposing \(K_m\) into \(k\) factors, each \(K_{s,t}\)-free. For \(s=t=2\) (\(G=C_4\)) and odd \(m=2k+1\), \(K_{2k+1}\) decomposes into \(k\) Hamilton cycles; each cycle has girth at least 5, hence is \(C_4\)-free. This shows \(R(K_{2,2};k) \geq 2k+2\). Separate case analysis establishes equality for small \(k\) (e.g., \(R(K_{2,2};2)=6\)). For \(s=2\) and general \(t\), Paley graphs of prime order \(q \equiv 1 \pmod{4}\) (or their complements) are strongly regular with parameters guaranteeing that every pair of vertices has at most \(t-2\) common neighbors when \(q=tk+1\); the resulting 2-colorings are \(K_{2,t}\)-free on \(tk+1\) vertices, matching the star lower bound when \(s=1\).

For \(\min(s,t) \geq 3\) the pattern breaks. The Clebsch graph on 16 vertices is strongly regular \((16,5,0,2)\) and both it and its complement are \(K_{3,3}\)-free; the corresponding 2-edge-coloring of \(K_{16}\) therefore contains no monochromatic \(K_{3,3}\). Hence \(R(K_{3,3};2) \geq 17\). (It is known that equality holds, but the proof relies on exhaustive computer search and stability arguments rather than a simple pigeonhole or inductive construction.) This already exceeds the linear expression \((s+t-2)k+2=10\) suggested by the star and \(C_4\) cases. Similar discrepancies appear for larger \(s,t\): lower-bound constructions utilizing finite geometries, random methods, or algebraic graphs often survive past \((s+t-2)k+1\) vertices, while upper bounds obtained by iteratively applying the pigeonhole principle to neighborhoods only recover the KST-order estimate, not an exact match.

Attempts at a uniform inductive proof on \(s+t+k\) fail to close. Suppose an inductive hypothesis that the exact value is known for all smaller parameters. Fixing a vertex \(v\) and letting \(N_i\) be its neighborhood in color \(i\), the sizes \(|N_i|\) sum to \(m-1\). Even if one \(|N_i|\) reaches the inductive threshold for \(R(K_{s-1,t};k)\) or \(R(K_{s,t-1};k)\), the monochromatic copy guaranteed inside \(N_i\) may appear in a color different from \(i\). Attaching \(v\) then produces a copy only when the colors coincide; otherwise one obtains a monochromatic copy of a strictly smaller bipartite graph, which does not advance the induction. Balancing the argument over all choices of which side receives \(v\) likewise yields only asymptotic bounds.

Because the lower-bound constructions become increasingly dependent on the existence of graphs with prescribed codegrees (or higher-order common-neighborhood bounds) and the upper bounds rely on case-by-case stability or computation for each pair \((s,t)\), no single closed-form expression valid for all \(s,t,k\) is known. Specific values have been determined only for very small parameters (e.g., all cases with \(\min(s,t)=1\), the pairs \((2,2)\), \((2,3)\), and a handful of others up to computer-assisted verification). The general determination of \(R(K_{s,t};k)\) remains open.
