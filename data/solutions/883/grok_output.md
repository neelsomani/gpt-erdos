The extremal quantity \(\lfloor n/2 \rfloor + \lfloor n/3 \rfloor - \lfloor n/6 \rfloor\) equals the size of the set \(B\) of integers in \(\{1, \dots, n\}\) divisible by 2 or by 3. Partition \(B = M_2 \cup M_3 \cup M_6\), where \(M_2\) consists of multiples of 2 but not 3, \(M_3\) consists of multiples of 3 but not 2, and \(M_6\) consists of multiples of 6. There are no edges of \(G(B)\) within \(M_2\), within \(M_3\), or within \(M_6\) (as all pairs in each part share a prime factor of 2 or 3). There are also no edges of \(G(B)\) between \(M_2\) and \(M_6\) (share factor 2) or between \(M_3\) and \(M_6\) (share factor 3). Thus all edges of \(G(B)\) (if any) lie between \(M_2\) and \(M_3\), so \(G(B)\) is bipartite (with isolated vertices from \(M_6\)) and contains no odd cycles.

Let \(C = \{1, \dots, n\} \setminus B\), so elements of \(C\) are coprime to 6. Any \(A\) with \(|A| > |B|\) must satisfy \(|A \cap C| \geq 1\). However, this alone does not immediately force all short odd cycles in \(G(A)\). To see why further analysis is delicate, consider the possible odd cycles in \(G(A)\). An odd cycle of length \(2k+1\) in \(G(A)\) is a sequence \(a_1, \dots, a_{2k+1} \in A\) such that \(\gcd(a_i, a_{i+1}) = 1\) for all \(i\) (indices mod \(2k+1\)) but the sequence cannot be 2-colored without a monochromatic edge. When elements of \(C\) are present in \(A\), they can connect to both \(M_2\) and \(M_3\) (provided no other prime factors are shared), potentially creating odd cycles that alternate between parts of \(B\) but "flip" parity via an element of \(C\).

For \(C_3\) (a triangle), this requires three elements of \(A\) that are pairwise coprime. If \(A\) contains an element \(z \in C\) along with sufficiently many elements of \(M_2\) and \(M_3\) (all coprime to \(z\)), then pairs from \(M_2\) and \(M_3\) that are themselves coprime yield triangles with \(z\). But quantifying "sufficiently many" to force this (and longer odd cycles) in every such \(A\) requires controlling the prime factors shared between elements of \(M_2 \cup M_3\) and \(z\). For a fixed odd length \(2k+1 \leq n/3 + 1\), one might hope to embed a cycle by selecting vertices whose prime factors are controlled (e.g., powers of distinct primes larger than \(2k+1\)), but ensuring this works uniformly over all large \(A\) (including those that avoid certain residue classes or concentrate on multiples of a fixed prime \(p > 3\)) appears to demand a sieve-theoretic density argument or dependent random choice to guarantee the required coprimality conditions.

The bound \(n/3 + 1\) on the cycle lengths is suggestive of the fact that \(|M_3| \approx n/3\), but it is unclear if this is tight or if a shorter uniform bound on cycle lengths holds. No obvious counterexample \(A\) (with \(|A| > |B|\) but some odd cycle of length at most \(n/3 + 1\) missing) is immediate: taking \(A = B \cup \{z\}\) for a single \(z \in C\) (e.g., \(z\) a prime larger than \(n/3\)) appears to create many short odd cycles via coprime pairs from \(M_2\) and \(M_3\), but proving that *every* odd length up to \(n/3 + 1\) arises would require showing that the neighborhoods \(N(z) \cap M_2\) and \(N(z) \cap M_3\) are rich enough to support cycle embeddings of all such lengths (e.g., via Dirac-type theorems in the bipartite graph between \(M_2\) and \(M_3\) induced by coprimality, after removing multiples of primes dividing \(z\)). Extending to all larger \(A\) only makes the graph denser, but the argument does not close rigorously without new extremal lemmas on coprimality graphs.

For the second question, a copy of the complete tripartite graph \(K_{1,\ell,\ell}\) in \(G(A)\) on vertex set \(\{v\} \cup X \cup Y\) (with \(|X| = |Y| = \ell\)) requires:
- \(\gcd(v, x) = 1\) for all \(x \in X \cup Y\),
- \(\gcd(x, y) = 1\) for all \(x \in X\), \(y \in Y\),
- \(\gcd(x_1, x_2) > 1\) for all distinct \(x_1, x_2 \in X\),
- \(\gcd(y_1, y_2) > 1\) for all distinct \(y_1, y_2 \in Y\).

(The non-edges of \(K_{1,\ell,\ell}\) are precisely the non-coprimality conditions within \(X\) and within \(Y\).) As \(G(B)\) is bipartite with the part structure above, it contains no such \(K_{1,\ell,\ell}\) for any \(\ell \geq 1\): any candidate \(v \in M_2\) has neighborhood contained in \(M_3\), forcing both \(X, Y \subseteq M_3\) (to satisfy the adjacency conditions from \(v\)), but then all pairs in \(X \cup Y\) fail to be coprime (contradicting the cross-condition between \(X\) and \(Y\)). Symmetric arguments rule out \(v \in M_3\) or \(v \in M_6\). Thus again \(B\) is extremal.

If \(A\) properly contains elements of \(C\), a candidate \(v \in A \cap C\) has potential neighborhoods in both \(M_2\) and \(M_3\). One could then try taking \(X \subseteq M_2\) and \(Y \subseteq M_3\) (satisfying the non-coprimality within each) such that the cross-coprimality between \(X\) and \(Y\) holds and \(v\) is coprime to all of \(X \cup Y\). For fixed \(\ell\), this reduces to finding large subsets of \(M_2\) and \(M_3\) whose elements' prime factors (all \(> 3\)) are disjoint from each other and from those of \(v\). When \(n\) is large relative to \(\ell\), taking \(X\) to be multiples of a fixed prime \(p > 3\) (with \(p \nmid v\)) and \(Y\) multiples of a fixed prime \(q > 3\) (with \(q \neq p\), \(q \nmid v\)) can work if the cross-pairs are coprime (true if no further factors are shared, which holds with positive density for large \(n\)). However, forcing this configuration in *every* large \(A\) (including those that omit all multiples of small primes or concentrate on numbers with many prime factors) requires a robust version of the Chinese Remainder Theorem to guarantee simultaneous coprimality and non-coprimality conditions, and this does not appear to follow from standard sieve methods.
