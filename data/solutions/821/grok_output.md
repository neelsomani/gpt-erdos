# Grok Response for Problem 821

**Problem 821.** Let \(g(n)\) count the number of \(m\) such that \(\phi(m)=n\). Is it true that, for every \(\epsilon>0\), there exist infinitely many \(n\) such that
\[
g(n) > n^{1-\epsilon}?
\]

After significant effort, considering possible constructions for large \(g(n)\) (via \(n\) highly divisible by small primorials to admit many primes \(p\) with \(p-1 \mid n\)), crude upper bounds (via the at-most \(\tau(n)\) candidate primes \(p\) with \(p-1 \mid n\), the constraint \(m = O(n \log \log n)\), and recursive decompositions over the largest prime factor of solutions \(m\)), inductive arguments on bounds of the form \(\exp(c \log n / \log \log n)\) or \(n^{o(1)}\), and Dickman-function estimates on the count of smooth preimages, I was unable to resolve the question rigorously in either direction.

I could not solve it.
