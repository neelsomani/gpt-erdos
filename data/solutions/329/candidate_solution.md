Let (A(N):=\lvert A\cap{1,\dots,N}\rvert). For any Sidon set (A\subseteq\mathbb N),

* **Upper bound.** One always has
  [
  \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{A(N)}{\sqrt N}\le 1,
  ]
  coming from the sharp finite extremal bounds for Sidon subsets of ({1,\dots,N}) [[nomath]](Erdős–Turán-type bounds imply $\max_{A\subseteq [N]\ \text{Sidon}}|A|=(1+o(1))\sqrt N$)[[/nomath]]. ([Erdős Problems][1])

* **Lower bounds (constructions).** Erdős showed there exists an infinite Sidon set with
  [
  \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{A(N)}{\sqrt N}\ge \frac12,
  ]
  and Krückeberg improved this to
  [
  \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{A(N)}{\sqrt N}\ge \frac1{\sqrt2}.
  ]
  ([Erdős Problems][1])

So, **the limsup can be at least (1/\sqrt2) and is at most $1$**:
[
\frac1{\sqrt2}\ \le\ \sup_{A\ \text{Sidon}}\ \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{A(N)}{\sqrt N}\ \le\ 1.
]
([Erdős Problems][1])

* **Status of the exact optimum.** Whether the upper bound $1$ can actually be achieved [[nomath]](i.e. whether there exists an infinite Sidon set with limsup $=1$)[[/nomath]] is a well-known Erdős conjecture / open problem. ([arXiv][2])
