# Grok Response for Problem 585

The problem asks for the maximum number of edges in an \(n\)-vertex graph \(G\) with no two edge-disjoint cycles \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) satisfying \(V(C_1) = V(C_2)\). (We consider only simple graphs.) Denote this extremal function by \(\operatorname{ex}(n, \mathcal{F})\), where \(\mathcal{F}\) is the family of all graphs containing such a pair of cycles.

First, observe that the forbidden configuration is only possible for \(|V(C_1)| = |V(C_2)| = k \geq 5\). For \(k = 3\), two edge-disjoint cycles would require 6 edges on 3 vertices, exceeding \(\binom{3}{2} = 3\). For \(k = 4\), two edge-disjoint 4-cycles require 8 edges, exceeding \(\binom{4}{2} = 6\). For \(k \geq 5\), the union of the two cycles is a 4-regular spanning subgraph on those \(k\) vertices (each vertex has degree exactly 4 in the union), so any such configuration on vertex set \(S\) with \(|S| = k \geq 5\) requires \(\delta(G[S]) \geq 4\) (and at least \(2k\) edges in \(G[S]\)).

A graph is 3-degenerate if every nonempty induced subgraph has a vertex of degree at most 3. Equivalently, such graphs have empty 4-core: repeatedly deleting a vertex of current degree at most 3 eventually removes all vertices. Equivalently (since the existence of any subgraph \(H\) with \(\delta(H) \geq 4\) on vertex set \(S\) implies \(\delta(G[S]) \geq 4\)), 3-degenerate graphs have no subgraph with minimum degree at least 4. A 3-degenerate graph on \(n \geq 4\) vertices has at most \(3n - 6\) edges, with equality achieved by the maximal 3-degenerate graphs (also called 3-trees): begin with \(K_4\) and iteratively add a new vertex of degree exactly 3 adjacent to the three vertices of some triangle in the current graph. Any such graph avoids the forbidden configuration (as \(\delta(G[S]) \leq 3\) for all \(|S| \geq 5\)).

However, \(3n - 6\) is not extremal. For \(n = 6\), the graph \(K_6\) minus a perfect matching has 12 edges and \(\delta = 4\), so it is not 3-degenerate; it admits two edge-disjoint Hamilton cycles (e.g., on vertices \(\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\) with missing edges \(\{1 2, 3 4, 5 6\}\), the cycles \(1-4-2-5-3-6-1\) and \(1-3-2-6-4-5-1\) are edge-disjoint). By contrast, \(K_6\) minus two disjoint edges has 13 edges. Every 5-vertex subset spans at most 9 edges (so cannot contain two edge-disjoint 5-cycles, as those require 10 edges). On all 6 vertices, suppose for a contradiction there exist two edge-disjoint 6-cycles. The four vertices incident to the missing edges have degree 4 in the graph, so both cycles must use all their incident edges. For any choice of one 6-cycle, the remaining available edges at these four vertices force all three remaining edges at each of the two degree-5 vertices to be included in the second cycle, yielding degree 3 at those vertices in the second cycle—a contradiction. Thus, this graph on 6 vertices has 13 edges and avoids the forbidden configuration, so \(\operatorname{ex}(6, \mathcal{F}) \geq 13 > 3 \cdot 6 - 6 = 12\).

In general, the presence of a set \(S\) with \(|S| \geq 5\) and \(\delta(G[S]) \geq 4\) is necessary but not sufficient for the forbidden configuration: \(G[S]\) must additionally contain two edge-disjoint Hamilton cycles. There exist graphs with \(\delta \geq 4\) on \(k \geq 9\) vertices that are non-Hamiltonian (hence have zero Hamilton cycles), and there exist Hamiltonian graphs with \(\delta \geq 4\) whose edges cannot be covered by two edge-disjoint Hamilton cycles. Constructions denser than \(3n-6\) edges can thus sometimes avoid the configuration by ensuring that every \(S\) with \(\delta(G[S]) \geq 4\) fails to have two edge-disjoint Hamilton cycles in \(G[S]\). However, standard sufficient conditions for Hamiltonicity (e.g., Dirac's theorem) or for multiple edge-disjoint Hamilton cycles (e.g., Nash-Williams theorems) become satisfied in sufficiently dense graphs, so there is a tradeoff: adding too many edges forces the configuration on some \(S\).

No construction achieving the maximum for all \(n\) is apparent, nor is a matching upper bound (e.g., via counting, stability, or discharging). The problem thus remains open.
