# Grok Response for Problem 951

**Problem 951.** We consider a strictly increasing sequence of real numbers \(1 < a_1 < a_2 < \cdots\) such that all distinct elements of the multiplicative semigroup
\[
S = \Bigl\{ \prod_{i} a_i^{k_i} : k = (k_i) \in \mathbb{N}_0^{(\mathbb{N})}, \text{ finitely supported} \Bigr\}
\]
(including the empty product \(1\)) satisfy \(|s - t| \geq 1\). The question is whether this forces the counting function \(A(x) = \#\{i : a_i \leq x\}\) to satisfy \(A(x) \leq \pi(x)\) for all \(x > 1\).

First note that the condition implies all elements of \(S\) are distinct (otherwise the difference is \(0 < 1\)). Ordering the elements of \(S \cap [1, N]\) as \(1 = s_1 < s_2 < \cdots < s_m \leq N\), the minimal-distance condition gives \(s_{k+1} \geq s_k + 1\), so \(s_k \geq k\). Thus \(m \leq N\), i.e.,
\[
|S \cap [1, N]| \leq N.
\]
In particular \(a_1 \geq 2\).

The sequence of all primes satisfies the hypothesis: the semigroup \(S\) is exactly the positive integers (by the fundamental theorem of arithmetic), which are distinct and satisfy \(|m - n| \geq 1\) for \(m \neq n\). For this choice we have equality \(A(x) = \pi(x)\), so the claimed bound is sharp if true.

To test whether larger cardinalities are possible, first consider integer-valued sequences. Suppose the \(a_i\) are integers \(\geq 2\). Then every element of \(S\) is an integer, so distinctness implies the distance condition. Each new generator introduces a new prime factor \(p\) (otherwise a multiplicative relation with earlier generators would produce a repeated value). The representation of any element of \(S\) in terms of the generators is unique when viewed in prime exponents: the valuation at each new prime recovers the exponent on the corresponding generator, after which the exponents on the initial primes can be recovered. Thus distinct tuples yield distinct integers, and the condition holds.

However, replacing a prime \(p\) by a composite multiple \(p \cdot q\) (with \(q\) already appearing in earlier generators) merely shifts the representative to a larger integer while still “using up” the prime \(p\). Since the representative is at least \(p\), choosing the composite makes the generator larger. Consequently, the maximal number of integer generators \(\leq x\) equals the maximal number of distinct primes that can be represented by integers \(\leq x\), which is achieved precisely by taking the primes themselves. In all explicit constructions (e.g., replacing 7 by 14 while keeping 2, 3, 5; replacing several primes by composites in gaps of the \(\{2,3\}\)-smooth numbers) one obtains at most \(\pi(x)\) generators \(\leq x\).

For non-integer generators the situation is more subtle. In gaps between consecutive elements \(s < t\) of the semigroup generated by the first few \(a_i\) (with \(t - s > 2\)), the open interval \((s+1, t-1)\) is free of distance-\(<1\) violations with existing elements. Inside such a gap one may attempt to place an additional real number \(a\) provided all new elements \(a^k \cdot u\) (\(u\) already in the semigroup, \(k \geq 1\)) lie at distance \(\geq 1\) from all existing elements. Equivalently, for every pair of existing elements \(u, v\) one must have
\[
\bigl| a^k \cdot u - v \bigr| \geq 1,
\]
i.e., \(a\) avoids a small interval of length \(2/u\) about each root \((v/u)^{1/k}\). There are countably many such forbidden intervals. Their total measure inside a fixed gap may be strictly less than the gap length (the widths decay like \(1/u\)), so non-empty complement may remain.

If a value \(a\) can be chosen in the complement, and if all higher-order Diophantine conditions (for large exponents) can be satisfied simultaneously, then an extra generator below \(x\) would be possible, potentially yielding \(A(x) > \pi(x)\). Linear forms in logarithms (Baker’s theorem and its variants) provide lower bounds on
\[
\bigl| k \log a + \sum m_i \log a_i \bigr|
\]
that are superpolynomial in the height but still larger than the exponentially small thresholds needed to force an absolute difference \(< 1\) for large elements of \(S\). Thus, for suitably chosen transcendental \(a\) (e.g., sufficiently independent of \(\log 2, \log 3, \ldots\)), it is plausible that all required distances remain \(\geq 1\).

Nevertheless, when many candidate generators (\(> \pi(x)\)) are placed in multiple gaps while simultaneously including all small primes, the system of Diophantine inequalities becomes overconstrained. The multiplicative relations among the new elements force clustering of high-degree products, violating the global distance condition. No explicit construction exceeding \(\pi(x)\) has been found, and the integer case suggests that the primes realize the extremal configuration.

After extensive case analysis for small primes, gap computations, measure estimates on forbidden intervals, and consideration of linear forms in logarithms, a rigorous proof that \(A(x) \leq \pi(x)\) (or a counterexample) could not be obtained.
