The problem asks whether every minimal \(k\)-basis \(A\) (in the stated sense) admits an infinite subset \(B\) whose removal leaves a \((k+1)\)-basis. While the minimality condition tightly constrains removals for order exactly \(k\), the extra summand for order \(k+1\) intuitively permits sparse infinite removals without destroying coverage of all sufficiently large integers. Explicit verification for \(k=1\) (cofinite \(A\)) succeeds by removing any sufficiently sparse infinite \(B\), as each large \(n\) retains representations \(a+b=n\) with both summands outside \(B\). Padding arguments using a fixed element of \(A\setminus B\) reduce the question to perturbed \(k\)-representations, but ensuring the perturbation avoids all critical tuples simultaneously for infinitely many \(n\) resists rigorous control without stronger uniformity on the representation functions. The minimality for order \(k\) supplies essentiality of infinite subsets but does not directly translate into non-essentiality for order \(k+1\). No contradiction arises from assuming the contrary, nor does any obvious counterexample construction. Thus the statement remains unresolved.
