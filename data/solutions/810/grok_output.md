# Grok Response for Problem 810

**I could not solve it.**

The problem asks whether there is a fixed \(\epsilon > 0\) such that, for all large \(n\), a graph \(G\) on \(n\) vertices with at least \(\epsilon n^2\) edges admits an \(n\)-edge-coloring in which every copy of \(C_4\) is rainbow (uses four distinct colors). After exploring a range of constructions and proof approaches, no decisive argument establishing existence or non-existence of such an \(\epsilon\) was obtained.

Constructions based on random graphs \(G(n,p)\) with \(p = \epsilon > 0\) fixed, equipped with random \(n\)-colorings, yield \(\Theta(n^4)\) copies of \(C_4\) in expectation while each fixed \(C_4\) fails to be rainbow with probability \(\Theta(1/n)\). The resulting expected number of non-rainbow \(C_4\)s is \(\Theta(n^3)\). Symmetric Lovász Local Lemma fails to eliminate them because the dependency degree per \(C_4\) is \(\Theta(n^2)\) (arising from the \(\Theta(n^2 p^3)\) other \(C_4\)s sharing an edge with a given one), producing an LLL product \(\omega(1)\).

Algebraic colorings on \(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\) (edge \(\{x,y\}\) colored by \(x+y \pmod{n}\) or by minimal difference) produce dense graphs but admit parallelogram-type \(C_4\)s on which only two colors appear. Blow-ups of small \(C_4\)-free graphs introduce \(C_4\)s under blow-up, and assigning colors consistently to keep all such rainbow again collapses to at most \(o(n^2)\) edges before repeated colors appear on some \(C_4\).

Bipartite constructions fare similarly. In a dense bipartite graph (\(\Omega(n^2)\) edges), the requirement that every \(2 \times 2\) biclique receives four distinct colors on its crossing edges forces each color class to be an induced matching: any two same-color edges, whether sharing a vertex or vertex-disjoint, complete to a \(C_4\) once the crossing edges are present, violating the rainbow condition. With only \(n\) colors the total edge count drops to \(O(n)\). Relaxing density to \(O(n^{3/2})\) (Zarankiewicz extremal) permits larger induced matchings per color and vacuously satisfies the condition in \(C_4\)-free graphs, but again falls short of \(\epsilon n^2\).

Upper-bound attempts via counting also stall. With \(n\) colors the \(\binom{n}{4}\) possible 4-tuples of colors must cover all \(C_4\)s rainbow-wise. Fixing any four colors, the subgraph they span can support at most \(O(n)\) rainbow \(C_4\)s when each monochromatic factor is a matching (as forced in dense bipartite examples), since the union then has bounded degree and a \(\Delta = O(1)\) graph contains \(O(n)\) copies of \(C_4\) (each vertex contributes \(O(1)\) candidate \(C_4\)s by choosing \(O(1)\) pairs of neighbors and checking common neighbors). Yet general (non-bipartite) color classes admit stars or triangles provided external common-neighbor conditions hold; bounding the global \(C_4\) count against \(\binom{n}{4} \cdot O(n)\) yields only the trivial \(o(n^4)\) upper bound on \(C_4\)s, too weak to contradict the \(\Omega(n^4)\) lower bound forced by \(\epsilon n^2\) edges.

No construction achieves \(\Omega(n^2)\) edges while permitting an \(n\)-coloring with all \(C_4\) rainbow, nor does any counting, algebraic, or probabilistic argument rule out such an \(\epsilon > 0\). The problem therefore remains open.
