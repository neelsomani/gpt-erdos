# Grok Response for Problem 87

The proposed inequality claims a near-tight lower bound on the diagonal Ramsey number \(R(G,G)\) (abbreviated \(R(G)\)) in terms of the clique Ramsey number \(R(k) = R(k,k)\), for all graphs \(G\) on any number of vertices with \(\chi(G) = k\). (If \(\omega(G) = k\), then \(G\) contains \(K_k\) as a subgraph, so \(R(G) \geq R(k)\) holds trivially and the claim follows immediately. Thus it suffices to consider graphs with \(\omega(G) < k = \chi(G)\).)

To prove the claim, it would be necessary to show that for any such \(G\), there is a 2-edge-coloring of \(K_m\) with \(m = \lfloor (1-\epsilon)^k R(k) \rfloor\) containing no monochromatic copy of \(G\). Since \(m < R(k)\), there exist 2-edge-colorings of \(K_m\) with no monochromatic \(K_k\), but it is unclear how to produce one that additionally avoids monochromatic \(G\) (for arbitrary \(G\)). Standard lower-bound constructions for \(R(k)\) (e.g., random colorings, or explicit ones such as quadratic-residue tournaments) yield color classes that are \(K_k\)-free but may have chromatic number much larger than \(k-1\). Any such color class with \(\chi > k-1\) must contain some subgraph \(H\) with \(\chi(H) = k\), but there is no evident reason this \(H\) must be (or contain) the specific input graph \(G\).

To disprove the claim, it would be necessary to exhibit (for infinitely many \(k\), and any fixed \(\epsilon > 0\)) at least one graph \(G_k\) with \(\chi(G_k) = k\) such that \(R(G_k) \leq (1-\epsilon)^k R(k)\). Graphs with \(\chi(G) = k > \omega(G)\) exist with arbitrarily large girth (Erdős, via the probabilistic method on \(G(n,p)\) with suitable \(p = p(n,k,g)\), followed by deletion of vertices to reduce maximum degree). For such \(G\), if \(|V(G)| = n\) is not too large relative to \(k\) (e.g., \(n \leq \exp(O(\sqrt{k}))\) suffices), then \(R(G) \geq n+1\) is only a weak lower bound. An upper bound on \(R(G)\) of the desired form does not follow from known general bounds such as the Chvátal–Rödl–Szemerédi–Trotter theorem (which gives \(R(G) \leq c(\Delta(G)) \cdot n\) for maximum degree \(\Delta(G)\), but requires \(\Delta(G) \geq k-1\) and yields a \(c(\Delta)\) too large to be useful here). Similarly, neither random colorings of \(K_m\) (with \(m = (1-\epsilon)^k R(k)\)) nor explicit \(K_k\)-free colorings of this order appear to provably avoid monochromatic copies of a specific high-girth \(G\).

An analogous argument applies to the stronger claim (with fixed \(c > 0\)): the same obstacles arise in both constructing a suitable coloring to establish the lower bound on \(R(G)\) and in exhibiting a explicit \(G\) to violate it. (Note that a weak unconditional lower bound \(R(G) > (k-1)^2\) does hold for all such \(G\), since the largest complete graph whose edges can be bipartitioned into two subgraphs each with chromatic number at most \(k-1\) has order exactly \((k-1)^2\): if the color classes for a proper \((k-1)\)-coloring of the red graph are \(V_1, \dots, V_{k-1}\) and those for blue are \(W_1, \dots, W_{k-1}\), then all edges within each \(V_i\) are blue and within each \(W_j\) are red, so \(|V_i \cap W_j| \leq 1\) for all \(i,j\) or a bichromatic edge arises, whence \(n \leq (k-1)^2\). But \((k-1)^2 = o((1-\epsilon)^k R(k))\) since \(R(k) > 2^{k/2}\) for large \(k\).)
