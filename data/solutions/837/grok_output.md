The definition of \(A_k\) encodes the values of \(\alpha\) at which there is a "jump" in the possible induced densities of large subhypergraphs of \(k\)-uniform hypergraphs. Specifically, \(\alpha \in A_k\) if there is some fixed \(\beta > \alpha\) such that density exceeding \(\alpha\) (in the \(\liminf\) sense along a sequence of hypergraphs \(G_n\) on \(|G_n| \to \infty\)) forces the existence of induced subhypergraphs \(H_n \subseteq G_n\) on \(|H_n| \to \infty\) whose densities have \(\liminf\) at least \(\beta\), but no such uniform \(\beta > \alpha\) can be forced if the hypothesis is weakened to \(\liminf\) at least \(\alpha\) (i.e., there exists a "extremal" sequence with densities at least \(\alpha\) whose large induced subhypergraphs cannot be forced above \(\alpha\) in the \(\liminf\) sense).

To determine membership in \(A_k\), one must identify all such jump points by establishing both a "forcing" result (any hypergraph sequence above \(\alpha\) admits a subsequence of induced subhypergraphs jumping to some strictly higher uniform \(\beta\)) and a matching "extremal" construction (a sequence at density exactly \(\alpha\) whose induced subhypergraphs on growing vertex sets stay at or below \(\alpha\) in the limit). For \(k = 2\), this can be resolved completely using the Erdős–Stone theorem and Turán graphs: if the \(\liminf\) exceeds \(1 - 1/r\) for integer \(r \geq 1\), then for large enough \(n\) the \(G_n\) contain large induced complete \((r+1)\)-partite subgraphs (with part sizes tending to infinity), which have induced density approaching \(1 - 1/(r+1) > 1 - 1/r\). Thus one may take \(\beta = 1 - 1/(r+1)\). The Turán graphs \(T(n, r)\) themselves form the required extremal sequence at density exactly \(1 - 1/r\), since every induced subgraph is \(r\)-partite and thus has density at most \(1 - 1/r\) in the limit. No other values of \(\alpha\) are jump points, since for \(\alpha\) strictly between \(1 - 1/r\) and \(1 - 1/(r+1)\), the forcing result for \(> 1 - 1/r\) already supplies a \(\beta > \alpha\), while the \(\geq \alpha\) version also holds (any sequence with densities \(\geq \alpha > 1 - 1/r\) eventually exceeds \(1 - 1/r\) by a fixed amount and thus forces the same jump). This yields \(A_2 = \{1 - 1/r : r = 1, 2, \dots \} \cap [0, 1]\).

For \(k = 3\), the situation is more subtle, as there is no direct analogue of the Erdős–Stone theorem, and hypergraph Turán problems remain open in general. One candidate jump point is \(\alpha = 3/4\), arising from the following extremal sequence. Partition the vertex set of \(G_n\) into parts \(A_n, B_n\) of sizes as equal as possible, and include as edges precisely the triples with exactly two vertices in one part and one in the other. The edge density is
\[
\frac{\binom{|A_n|}{2}|B_n| + \binom{|B_n|}{2}|A_n|}{\binom{n}{3}} \to \frac{3}{4}
\]
as \(n \to \infty\). For any induced subhypergraph on a vertex subset with proportions \(y\) from \(A_n\) and \(1-y\) from \(B_n\) (in the limit), the induced density is exactly \(3y(1-y) \leq 3/4\), with equality only in the balanced case \(y = 1/2\). Thus this sequence has \(\liminf\) exactly \(3/4\), but no induced subhypergraphs on growing vertex sets can have \(\liminf > 3/4\). This shows the "\(\geq \alpha\)" version of the property fails at \(\alpha = 3/4\) (for any candidate \(\beta > 3/4\)).

To show \(3/4 \in A_3\), it remains to prove a forcing result: there must exist some \(\beta > 3/4\) such that *every* sequence with \(\liminf > 3/4\) admits induced \(H_n\) on \(|H_n| \to \infty\) with \(\liminf > \beta\). No such forcing result is known (even for \(\beta\) only slightly larger than \(3/4\)), as it would require a stability theorem establishing that hypergraphs exceeding density \(3/4\) must contain large subsets whose induced structure forces a jump to a uniformly higher density (e.g., via blow-ups of denser small hypergraphs or partite constructions with more parts, such as the "non-monochromatic triples" construction on three equal parts, which achieves density \(8/9 > 3/4\) with no induced subhypergraphs exceeding \(8/9\)). Similar issues arise for other candidate values such as \(0\) (where random-like constructions with vanishing density can avoid uniform jumps to a fixed positive \(\beta\), but constructions like the above partite example suggest possible jumps to \(3/4\)) and values such as \(4/9\) or \(5/9\) (arising from Lagrangian considerations on certain 3-uniform hypergraphs, but without known matching forcing theorems).

Other values of \(\alpha \in (0, 1)\) are similarly unresolved. Constructions based on quasirandom hypergraphs, random blow-ups, or solutions to Lagrangian optimization problems suggest that jumps fail to occur for many \(\alpha > 0\) (consistent with the Frankl–Rödl theorem establishing the existence of non-jump points for all \(k \geq 3\)), but no complete characterization is available. In particular, it is not known whether \(A_3\) is finite, countable, an interval, or equal to \(\{3/4\}\) (or some superset thereof).
