# Grok Response for Problem 86

**Problem 86.** We consider the hypercube graph \(Q_n\), which is the graph with vertex set \(\{0,1\}^n\) (so \(|V(Q_n)| = 2^n\)) and edges between vertices differing in exactly one coordinate (so \(|E(Q_n)| = n \cdot 2^{n-1}\)). A subgraph \(G \subseteq Q_n\) is \(C_4\)-free if it contains no 4-cycle. All \(C_4\) subgraphs of \(Q_n\) arise as 2-faces: for any pair of distinct directions \(i, j \in [n]\) and any fixing of the remaining \(n-2\) coordinates, the four vertices spanned by flips in \(i\) and \(j\) induce a copy of \(C_4\).

There are exactly \(\binom{n}{2} \cdot 2^{n-2}\) such potential \(C_4\)s. The edges of \(Q_n\) can be partitioned by direction: for each \(i \in [n]\), there is a perfect matching \(M_i\) of \(2^{n-1}\) edges all flipping coordinate \(i\). Let \(e_i = |E(G) \cap M_i|\) (so the number of edges in \(G\) is \(e = \sum_{i=1}^n e_i\)), and let \(p_i = e_i / 2^{n-1}\) be the fraction of \(M_i\) included in \(G\).

For any fixed pair \(\{i,j\}\), the \(2^{n-2}\) copies of \(C_4\) using directions \(i\) and \(j\) partition the edges in \(M_i \cup M_j\). Each such \(C_4\) has four edges (two from each matching). To avoid a \(C_4\), \(G\) can include at most three edges from each such copy. Summing over all \(2^{n-2}\) copies for this pair yields the necessary condition
\[
e_i + e_j \leq 3 \cdot 2^{n-2} = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 2^{n-1},
\]
or equivalently \(p_i + p_j \leq 3/2\). (This bound is tight for a given pair if and only if \(G\) includes *exactly* three edges from *every* one of the \(2^{n-2}\) copies.) These \(\binom{n}{2}\) linear inequalities, together with \(0 \leq p_i \leq 1\), form an LP relaxation whose optimum is \(\sum p_i = (3/4)n\) (achieved by setting all \(p_i = 3/4\)). This would yield a \(C_4\)-free subgraph with \((3/4) \cdot n \cdot 2^{n-1}\) edges. Equivalently, letting \(f_i = 2^{n-1} - e_i\) (edges omitted from \(M_i\)), tightness requires exactly \(f_i = 2^{n-3}\) omissions per direction (for \(n \geq 3\)), with the omissions forming an *exact hitting set* for the collection of all 2-faces: each 2-face must be hit by *exactly one* omitted edge. Each omitted edge lies in exactly \(n-1\) faces (one for each choice of second direction), and the total number of faces is \(\binom{n}{2} 2^{n-2}\), so the parameters are consistent:
\[
n \cdot 2^{n-3} \cdot (n-1) = \binom{n}{2} \cdot 2^{n-2} \cdot 2.
\]

To check feasibility of all \(p_i = 3/4\), consider restrictions to 3-dimensional subcubes. There are \(\binom{n}{3} 2^{n-3}\) such subcubes (choose the three active directions, then fix each of the remaining \(n-3\) coordinates to 0 or 1). Each is isomorphic to \(Q_3\) (the cube graph, with 12 edges and 6 faces). Each edge of \(Q_n\) lies in exactly \(\binom{n-1}{2}\) such subcubes (choose two additional directions out of the remaining \(n-1\); the fixed coordinates for the others are forced by the edge). If \(G\) has at most 9 edges in *every* 3-subcube (the maximum for \(Q_3\), achieved by omitting exactly one edge per direction in a configuration hitting all six faces exactly once), double-counting yields
\[
e \cdot \binom{n-1}{2} \leq 9 \cdot \binom{n}{3} 2^{n-3}.
\]
Simplifying the right-hand side recovers exactly \(e \leq (3/4) n \cdot 2^{n-1}\), so the 3-subcube constraints are consistent with (but do not improve on) the pairwise bounds. Moreover, each 3-subcube must itself realize *exactly* the tight configuration (one omission per direction, with locations differing in all three relative coordinates so that the two faces per pair of directions are each hit exactly once).

For small \(n\), tight constructions exist. For \(n=2\) (\(Q_2 = C_4\)), omitting any one edge yields 3 edges (fraction \(3/4\)). For \(n=3\), omitting the specific edges \(\{000-100, 001-011, 110-111\}\) (one per direction) hits all six faces exactly once, yielding 9 edges (fraction \(3/4\)) with no \(C_4\). For \(n=4\), the antipodal pairs \(\{x, x+\mathbf{1}\}\) (where \(\mathbf{1}\) is the all-1s vector) partition \(V(Q_4)\) into 8 pairs; these correspond to the elements of the quotient space \(\mathbb{F}_2^4 / \langle \mathbf{1} \rangle \cong \mathbb{F}_2^3\). Each tight quadruple of vertices for a direction \(i\) (two omitted edges from \(M_i\)) corresponds to pairing two antipodal pairs that differ by translation by \(e_i\). Choosing representatives \(a_1 = 0\) (difference \(f_1\)), \(a_2 = f_3\) (difference \(f_2\)), \(a_3 = f_1 + f_2\) (difference \(f_3\)), \(a_4 = f_2\) (difference \(f_4 = f_1 + f_2 + f_3\)) in the quotient partitions the 8 pairs with no overlaps and uses each direction exactly once. The corresponding 8 omissions (two per direction) thus hit every one of the 24 faces of \(Q_4\) exactly once, yielding a \(C_4\)-free subgraph with 24 edges (fraction \(3/4\)).

For general \(n\), feasibility of the tight omissions (equivalently, partitioning the antipodal structure consistently with the per-3-subcube conditions) is equivalent to the existence of a global choice of "complementary" positions in each direction satisfying all local affine conditions over \(\mathbb{F}_2^n\). While consistent for \(n \leq 4\), compatibility for large \(n\) is unclear: the conditions over overlapping 3-subcubes impose a system of constraints on placements in each \(\mathbb{F}_2^{n-1}\) (positions orthogonal to \(e_i\)) that may not admit a simultaneous solution, potentially forcing some \(p_i + p_j < 3/2\) on average and reducing the feasible \(\sum p_i\) below \((3/4)n\).

A weaker construction is always feasible: set one \(p_1 = 1\) (include all of \(M_1\)) and all other \(p_i = 1/2\) (include half of each remaining \(M_i\), chosen to hit at most three edges per square with directions involving 1). The pairwise bounds hold (\(1 + 1/2 = 3/2\)), and this is realizable by taking appropriate halvings (e.g., conditioned on a linear functional orthogonal to \(e_1\)). This yields
\[
e = 2^{n-1} + (n-1) \cdot 2^{n-2} = \frac{n+1}{2} \cdot 2^{n-1} = \left( \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2n} \right) n \cdot 2^{n-1},
\]
which is \((1/2 + O(1/n))\)-fraction of the edges in \(Q_n\) and \(C_4\)-free. Thus, there exist \(C_4\)-free subgraphs with at least \((1/2 + \Omega(1/n))\) fraction of the edges.

The question asks whether \((1/2 + o(1))\) is nevertheless an asymptotic threshold (i.e., whether the maximum fraction of edges in a \(C_4\)-free subgraph is \(1/2 + o(1)\)). The LP and small-\(n\) constructions suggest a possible constant \(3/4\), but the compatibility of tight omissions for all \(n\) remains unresolved, as do tighter upper bounds from larger subcubes or eigenvalue methods.
