# Grok Response for Problem 851

**Problem 851.** We interpret "at most \(r\) prime divisors" as \(\omega(n) \leq r\) (distinct prime divisors). Let \(S_r\) be the set of positive integers \(m\) for which there exists \(k \geq 0\) with \(m > 2^k\) and \(\omega(m - 2^k) \leq r\). The question is whether, for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), there exists \(r = r(\varepsilon)\) (with the dependence expressed by the given notation) such that the lower asymptotic density of \(S_r\) is at least \(1 - \varepsilon\).

To analyze this, consider the complement: an integer \(m\) lies outside \(S_r\) (is "bad") if \(\omega(m - 2^k) > r\) for all \(0 \leq k \leq \lfloor \log_2 m \rfloor\). For \(m \approx X\), there are \(\Theta(\log X)\) relevant values of \(k\).

A covering argument produces a positive lower density subset of bad integers for each fixed \(r\). Specifically, one seeks a finite collection of square-free odd moduli \(Q_i\) (each the product of exactly \(r+1\) distinct primes, so that divisibility by \(Q_i\) forces at least \(r+1\) distinct prime factors) together with residues \(a_i\) such that:
- the congruences \(k \equiv a_i \pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{Q_i}(2)}\) form a covering system of \(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\),
- the conditions \(m \equiv 2^{a_i} \pmod{Q_i}\) are compatible.

If the \(Q_i\) are pairwise coprime, CRT combines them into a single modulus \(M = \prod Q_i\). The resulting arithmetic progression(s) modulo \(M\) have density \(1/M > 0\). For all but \(o(X)\) members \(m \leq X\) of these progressions (excluding those within distance \(\max Q_i\) of a power of 2, of which there are \(O((\max Q_i) \log X)\) up to \(X\)), we have \(m - 2^k > \max Q_i\) for every relevant \(k\), so divisibility by the corresponding \(Q_i\) indeed forces \(\omega(m - 2^k) \geq r+1\). Thus, the lower density of bad integers is at least \(1/M > 0\), and the upper density of \(S_r\) is at most \(1 - 1/M < 1\).

Coverings exist for every fixed \(r\) (by bundling \(r+1\) primes into each \(Q_i\) of a known covering system for orders of 2, or by extending known explicit coverings as in the Sierpiński problem and its variants). Moreover, coverings can be chosen so that \(M = M(r) \to \infty\) as \(r \to \infty\) (by incorporating sufficiently many distinct primes, with orders of 2 realizing a covering system of \(\mathbb{Z}\)). This makes the lower bound \(1/M(r)\) on the bad density arbitrarily small for large \(r\).

A naive probabilistic model (treating the \(m - 2^k\) as independent random integers of size \(\approx m\), using that the density of integers \(y \leq Y\) with \(\omega(y) \leq r\) is \(\asymp (\log\log Y)^{r-1}/((r-1)! \log Y)\)) suggests an expected number of good \(k\) (with \(\omega(m-2^k) \leq r\)) of size \(\asymp (\log\log m)^{r-1}\). For \(r \geq 2\) this tends to infinity, and averaging \(\exp(-c(\log\log m)^{r-1})\) up to \(X\) tends to 0, suggesting density of \(S_r\) tends to 1. However, the covering shows this model fails: arithmetic progressions exist in which modular constraints force *all* relevant \(m - 2^k\) to be divisible by fixed small primes, inducing strong dependencies that invalidate independence assumptions. (The covering holds uniformly in \(k\), but only the \(O(\log m)\) relevant terms matter for each \(m\), and exceptions near powers of 2 affect only density \(o(1)\).)

The covering yields only a lower bound on the lower density of the bad set (hence an upper bound on the density of \(S_r\)). To affirm the existence of \(r(\varepsilon)\) making the *lower* density of \(S_r\) at least \(1-\varepsilon\), an upper bound \(\leq \varepsilon\) on the upper density of bad integers is required. No such bound follows from the covering (which can be made arbitrarily weak by enlarging \(M(r)\)). Bounding the bad set from above appears to require controlling the simultaneous distribution of \(\omega(m - 2^k)\) over \(\Theta(\log m)\) correlated shifts (differing by differences of the form \(2^\ell(2^j - 1)\)), likely needing strong equidistribution or sieve estimates modulo products of the \(Q_i\) that grow with \(r\). Existing techniques for almost-prime values in thin sets do not directly apply, as the shifts depend on \(m\) itself.

After extensive examination of the covering construction, heuristics, and dependencies, the problem remains unresolved: it is consistent with the above that the lower density of \(S_r\) tends to 1 as \(r \to \infty\) (with the covering providing a vanishing but positive lower bound on the bad density), but no rigorous proof that the bad upper density can be made arbitrarily small for large fixed \(r = r(\varepsilon)\) is available, nor is there a disproof via a uniform positive lower density independent of \(r\).

Thus, I could not solve it.
