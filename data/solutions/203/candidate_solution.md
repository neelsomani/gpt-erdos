If you really mean **any integer** $m$ and “prime” means a **(positive) prime number**, then there is a trivial example:

* Take (m=-1). Then ((m,6)=1), and for all (k,\ell\ge 0),
  [
  2^k3^\ell m+1 ;=; 1-2^k3^\ell ;\le; 0,
  ]
  so none of these values is a (positive) prime.

However, in the **intended number-theoretic sense** (as in the Erdős–Graham question), one is asking for a **positive** integer $m$ with $(m,6)=1$. Under that interpretation:

* **This is an open problem.** No such positive $m$ is currently known, and it is explicitly listed as open (Erdős Problem #203). ([Erdős Problems][1])

For context, the one-parameter analogue (“Sierpiński numbers”) asks for odd $m$ such that $m2^n+1$ is composite for all $n$, and such numbers are known (e.g. (78557)). ([Erdős Problems][1])
