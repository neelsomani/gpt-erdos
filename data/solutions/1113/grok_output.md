# Grok Response for Problem 1113

**Problem 1113.** We work throughout with a fixed odd positive integer \(m\). Let \(a_k = m \cdot 2^k + 1\) for \(k \geq 0\). The integer \(m\) is called a Sierpiński number if \(a_k\) is composite for every \(k \geq 0\). A finite set \(P\) of primes is a covering set for \(m\) if, for every \(k \geq 0\), there exists \(p \in P\) such that \(p \mid a_k\).

The question is whether there exists a Sierpiński number \(m\) possessing no covering set.

Assume for contradiction that every Sierpiński number admits a covering set. We will derive a structural property that appears too rigid to exhaust all possibilities forced by the Chinese Remainder Theorem.

Fix any finite set \(Q = \{q_1, \dots, q_r\}\) of distinct odd primes. For each \(q_i\) let \(d_i\) be the multiplicative order of 2 modulo \(q_i\). The congruence
\[
m \cdot 2^k \equiv -1 \pmod{q_i}
\]
is solvable for \(k\) if and only if \(-m\) lies in the subgroup generated by 2 in \((\mathbb{Z}/q_i\mathbb{Z})^\times\). When solvable, the solutions \(k\) form a union of at most \(\varphi(d_i)/d_i\) arithmetic progressions modulo \(d_i\). Collecting these progressions over all \(q_i \in Q\) produces a (possibly empty) system of simultaneous congruences on \(k\). Let \(\Delta_Q\) be the least common multiple of the moduli appearing in this system. Then the set of all \(k\) for which \(a_k\) is divisible by at least one prime of \(Q\) is a union of at most \(2^{\Delta_Q}\) residue classes modulo \(\Delta_Q\).

If \(Q\) is a covering set for \(m\), the union must be all of \(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\). In particular the density of covered \(k\) is 1. Now suppose \(m\) is Sierpiński but admits no covering set. Then for every finite \(Q\) the set
\[
K_Q = \{k \geq 0 : \gcd(a_k, \prod_{p\in Q} p) = 1\}
\]
is infinite. On \(K_Q\) every \(a_k\) must be composite yet free of prime factors from \(Q\). Write \(a_k = b_k c_k\) with \(b_k, c_k > 1\) both coprime to \(\prod Q\). Since \(a_k \sim m \cdot 2^k\), at least one of \(b_k, c_k\) tends to infinity along \(K_Q\).

Consider the Dirichlet density of primes \(p\) for which the order of 2 modulo \(p\) divides some fixed multiple of \(\Delta_Q\). By the Chebotarev density theorem applied to the splitting of cyclotomic extensions, the set of primes \(p \nmid 2m\) such that \(2\) has order dividing \(\Delta_Q\) modulo \(p\) has positive density (bounded away from zero independently of \(m\) once \(\Delta_Q\) is fixed). For any such \(p\) the condition \(m \cdot 2^k \equiv -1 \pmod{p}\) either has no solution or else holds on a positive-density set of \(k\) (density at least \(1/(2\Delta_Q)\)). Thus, if we enlarge \(Q\) by adjoining one new prime from each such Chebotarev class, we can force additional residue classes modulo a larger \(\Delta\) to be covered.

Iterating this enlargement yields an infinite ascending chain of moduli \(\Delta_1 \mid \Delta_2 \mid \cdots\) and corresponding finite sets \(Q_1 \subset Q_2 \subset \cdots\) such that the proportion of uncovered residue classes modulo \(\Delta_n\) tends to zero. By compactness of the profinite completion \(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\), there exists a coherent system of residue classes that is covered at every finite stage. The corresponding \(m\) modulo the product of all primes appearing in all \(Q_n\) can be chosen by the Chinese Remainder Theorem so that every \(a_k\) is divisible by at least one prime from \(\bigcup Q_n\).

This construction produces a Sierpiński number, but the covering set \(\bigcup Q_n\) is infinite. The assumption that every Sierpiński number admits a finite covering set therefore implies that the inverse limit process must stabilize after finitely many steps for some choice of primes. However, the Chebotarev densities are independent of the particular \(m\) chosen at each stage; one can always select a fresh prime \(p\) whose Frobenius conjugacy class forces a new uncovered arithmetic progression to appear in \(K_Q\) while keeping previous congruences intact. This prevents stabilization.

The contradiction shows that the assumption is false: there must exist Sierpiński numbers possessing no finite covering set.

(The argument above is incomplete; the final stabilization step relies on an effective version of Chebotarev that controls the smallest prime in each class relative to \(\Delta_n\), which is not known in full generality. The obstruction is therefore genuine and the problem remains open. After exhaustive examination of covering densities, profinite limits, and Chebotarev constraints I was unable to repair the gap.)
