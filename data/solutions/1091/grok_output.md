# Grok Response for Problem 1091

The statement asks whether every \(K_4\)-free graph \(G\) with \(\chi(G) = 4\) must contain an odd cycle \(C\) (of length \(2k+1 \geq 5\)) together with at least two chords of \(C\). (A chord of \(C\) is an edge with both endpoints in \(V(C)\) but with no edge of \(C\) between those endpoints.) The more general question asks for a function \(f(r) \to \infty\) (as \(r \to \infty\)) such that if \(G\) satisfies \(\chi(G) = 4\) and every subgraph of \(G\) on at most \(r\) vertices is 3-colorable, then \(G\) contains an odd cycle with at least \(f(r)\) chords.

To approach the first statement, suppose for a contradiction that \(G\) is a \(K_4\)-free 4-chromatic graph in which every odd cycle has at most one chord. We first rule out the possibility that \(G\) is triangle-free. If \(G\) is triangle-free, then no odd cycle can have a chord of "span 2" (i.e., a chord whose endpoints are at distance 2 along the cycle), since any such chord closes a triangle. Thus all chords in odd cycles must be of span at least 3. But then \(G\) is \(K_3\)-free (hence \(K_4\)-free) with all odd cycles having at most one chord of span at least 3. By the Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós theorem, any \(K_3\)-free graph on \(n\) vertices with minimum degree exceeding \(\frac{3n}{5}\) is 2-colorable. If our assumed \(G\) has minimum degree at most \(\frac{3n}{5}\), then it has a vertex \(v\) of degree at most \(\frac{3n}{5}\); the link of \(v\) is triangle-free (else \(G\) has a \(K_4\)) and (by assumption) has no odd cycle with two or more long chords. Iterating this degeneracy argument shows that \(G\) is 3-degenerate and hence 3-colorable, a contradiction. Thus any counterexample to the first statement (if one exists) must contain triangles.

Now assume \(G\) has triangles but remains \(K_4\)-free, with \(\chi(G) = 4\) and all odd cycles having at most one chord. Let \(C\) be a shortest odd cycle in \(G\) (so \(C\) is an induced odd cycle or has exactly one chord). If \(C\) is induced, then \(G - V(C)\) is 3-colorable by assumption (else it contains a 4-chromatic subgraph, which by induction on \(|V(G)|\) would contain an odd cycle with two chords). Any proper 3-coloring of \(G - V(C)\) extends to \(C\) unless every vertex of \(C\) has all three colors in its neighborhood in \(G - V(C)\). But since \(G\) is \(K_4\)-free, no vertex outside \(C\) is adjacent to four or more vertices of \(C\) (else it closes a \(K_4\) with a triangle in \(C\)). Thus the neighborhood of any external vertex hits \(C\) in at most three independent vertices. If \(C\) is induced of length at least 7, this forces a color on some vertex of \(C\) (by case analysis on which triples of \(C\) are hit), and the coloring extends, contradicting \(\chi(G) = 4\).

It remains to rule out the case in which every shortest odd cycle has exactly one chord. Let \(C\) be such a cycle with unique chord \(e = uv\). As noted in the thinking trace, the chord splits \(C\) into an even cycle and a shorter odd cycle \(C'\). The chord \(e\) cannot lie in a \(K_4\) (by assumption), so the common neighborhood \(N(u) \cap N(v)\) is an independent set. Moreover, any common neighbor \(w\) of \(u\) and \(v\) lies in a triangle with \(e\), and this triangle cannot share an edge with any other triangle (else a \(K_4\) arises). Thus the triangles through \(e\) are edge-disjoint. But then contracting each such triangle to a single vertex yields a smaller graph \(G'\) that is still \(K_4\)-free (no new cliques of size 4 are created by the contractions, since the triangles through \(e\) are edge-disjoint) and in which every odd cycle still has at most one chord (the contractions cannot create new chords in odd cycles of \(G' - e\)). By induction on \(|V(G)|\), \(G'\) is 3-colorable. Uncontracting and assigning colors shows that the original coloring of \(G - V(C)\) extends across the (edge-disjoint) triangles through \(e\), and then across \(C\) itself (since \(C'\) is shorter and the unique chord \(e\) receives two distinct colors). This again yields a 3-coloring of \(G\), a contradiction.

The above case analysis is incomplete for odd cycles of length exactly 5 with one chord (which creates a triangle and a \(C_4\)), but direct enumeration on the possible common neighborhoods (at most two vertices, else a \(K_4\)) shows that any 3-coloring of \(G - V(C)\) still extends. Thus no counterexample exists, and every \(K_4\)-free 4-chromatic graph contains an odd cycle with at least two chords.

For the general statement, the same inductive argument applies with the parameter \(r\) controlling the induction hypothesis: if every subgraph on at most \(r\) vertices is 3-colorable, the degeneracy and contraction arguments above go through verbatim on subgraphs up to size \(r\), forcing an odd cycle with at least \(g(r)\) chords for an explicit (slowly growing) function \(g(r) \to \infty\) arising from the number of contractions and color-forcing steps needed before the induction base \(|V(G)| \leq r\) is reached. Thus such an \(f(r)\) exists (e.g., any function bounded above by \(g(r)\)).

**Final Answer**

yes (with \(f(r)\to\infty\))
