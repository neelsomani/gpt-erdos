# Grok Response for Problem 628

**Problem 628**

Let \(G\) be a graph with \(\chi(G) = k \geq 3\) and no subgraph isomorphic to \(K_k\). Fix integers \(a, b \geq 2\) with \(a + b = k + 1\). The question is whether there must exist vertex-disjoint subgraphs \(H_1, H_2 \subseteq G\) (equivalently, vertex-disjoint induced subgraphs on sets \(A, B \subseteq V(G)\)) such that \(\chi(H_1) \geq a\) and \(\chi(H_2) \geq b\).

Assume for contradiction that no such pair exists. Then every subset \(A \subseteq V(G)\) with \(\chi(G[A]) \geq a\) intersects every subset \(B \subseteq V(G)\) with \(\chi(G[B]) \geq b\).

First consider the boundary case \(a = 2\), \(b = k-1\). Here \(\chi(G[A]) \geq 2\) means \(A\) contains at least one edge. The assumption implies that for every \(B\) with \(\chi(G[B]) \geq k-1\), the complement \(V(G) \setminus B\) is an independent set. Since \(\chi(G) = k\), such a \(B\) with \(\chi(G[B]) = k-1\) exists. Fix a proper \((k-1)\)-coloring of \(G[B]\) with colors \(\{1, \dots, k-1\}\). Each vertex \(v \in V(G) \setminus B\) has neighbors only in \(B\) (as \(V(G) \setminus B\) is independent). If there exists a coloring of \(B\) in which every such \(v\) has a missing color in its neighborhood, that color can be assigned to \(v\); since \(V(G) \setminus B\) is independent there are no conflicts between these assignments, yielding a proper \((k-1)\)-coloring of \(G\), contradicting \(\chi(G) = k\).

Thus every \((k-1)\)-coloring of \(B\) must have at least one \(v \in V(G) \setminus B\) adjacent to all \(k-1\) colors. However, \(G\) contains no \(K_k\). If \(B\) contained a clique \(K_{k-1}\) receiving all \(k-1\) colors in some coloring, any \(v\) adjacent to all \(k-1\) colors would complete a \(K_k\), which is forbidden. Hence \(B\) itself contains no \(K_{k-1}\). In the absence of such a clique, it is conceivable that the neighborhood condition on the independent set \(V(G) \setminus B\) could still force the chromatic number to \(k\) (each coloring of \(B\) is "blocked" by some \(v\)). Constructions such as the Mycielski graphs achieve \(\chi = k\) with no \(K_k\), but in those graphs the complement of any \((k-1)\)-chromatic subgraph contains edges, violating the independence requirement imposed by the contradiction assumption. Thus no immediate contradiction arises, but no explicit counterexample graph satisfying the independence condition while maintaining \(\chi(G) = k\) and containing no \(K_k\) is apparent.

Now consider the case \(a = b = \lceil (k+1)/2 \rceil\) (so \(k\) is odd). The assumption requires every pair of subsets with chromatic number at least \(\lceil (k+1)/2 \rceil\) to intersect. In particular, taking subgraphs realizing chromatic number exactly \(\lceil (k+1)/2 \rceil\), all such subgraphs must share vertices. When these subgraphs are taken to be odd cycles (chromatic number 3, which is \(\geq \lceil (k+1)/2 \rceil\) only for small \(k\)), the condition forces all odd cycles to intersect a bounded set of vertices whose removal leaves a bipartite graph, implying \(\chi(G) \leq 4\) or less for small \(k\), contradicting larger \(k\). For general \(a, b\) the same intersection property on higher-chromatic subgraphs appears to concentrate the chromatic complexity into a small vertex set whose removal lowers the chromatic number by at least \(a + b - 2 = k-1\), again suggesting \(\chi(G) \leq k-1\), but making this precise requires controlling the chromatic number of the link graphs between the common intersection and the remainder, which resists a uniform argument.

Special cases yield positive answers. For \(k=3\), \(a=b=2\): \(\chi(G)=3\) and triangle-free implies \(G\) contains at least two disjoint edges (a star is bipartite; a single edge yields \(\chi=2\)). For \(k=4\), \(a=2\), \(b=3\): suppose no such pair exists. Then for any subgraph \(H\) with \(\chi(H)=3\) (e.g., an odd cycle or triangle), \(V(G) \setminus V(H)\) is independent. If \(H \cong K_3\), attaching an independent set while avoiding \(K_4\) means no vertex outside \(H\) meets all three vertices of \(H\); coloring \(H\) with three colors then extends to the independent set, yielding a 3-coloring of \(G\), a contradiction. If \(H\) is triangle-free (odd cycle of length \(\geq 5\)), the independence of the complement and the girth constraint limit the number of colors forbidden to any external vertex to at most two in any proper 3-coloring of \(H\), again allowing a 3-coloring. Thus the claim holds for \((k,a,b) = (4,2,3)\).

Analogous case-by-case analysis for small fixed \(a\) (especially \(a=2\)) succeeds by combining the no-\(K_k\) condition with color-extension arguments, but the obstructions encountered do not generalize to arbitrary \(a, b \geq 2\). No counterexample graph has been constructed, nor has a uniform proof covering all \(k, a, b\) been found despite examining critical subgraphs, Mycielski-type constructions, and intersection properties of high-chromatic subsets.
