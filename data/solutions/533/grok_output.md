# Grok Response for Problem 533

**Problem 533.** Let \(\delta > 0\). If \(n\) is sufficiently large and \(G\) is a graph on \(n\) vertices with no \(K_5\) and at least \(\delta n^2\) edges, then \(G\) contains a set of \(\gg_\delta n\) vertices that induces a triangle-free subgraph.

I considered several approaches to proving the statement.

First, a direct application of the probabilistic deletion method fails to deliver a linear-size set. Let \(T\) be the number of triangles in \(G\). Since \(G\) is \(K_5\)-free, the neighborhood of any vertex induces a \(K_4\)-free graph, so by Turán's theorem the number of edges in \(N(v)\) is at most \((1/3+o(1))d(v)^2\). Thus \(T = O(n^3)\). Selecting a random subset \(S\) of size \(m\) yields \(\mathbb{E}[|S|] = m\) and \(\mathbb{E}[\text{number of triangles in }G[S]] = O(m^3)\). Deleting one vertex from each surviving triangle produces an expected size of \(m - O(m^3)\). Balancing terms forces \(m = O(1)\) to keep the subtracted term smaller than \(m/2\), which is useless.

A regularity-lemma approach also falls short of a linear bound. Apply Szemerédi's regularity lemma with \(\varepsilon = \varepsilon(\delta) > 0\) small to obtain an equitable partition into \(k = k(\varepsilon)\) clusters (with \(k\) bounded by a function of \(\varepsilon\)). Construct the meta-graph \(M\) on \([k]\) by placing an edge when the corresponding pair is \(\varepsilon\)-regular with density greater than a positive threshold \(\beta(\delta)\). The assumption \(e(G) \ge \delta n^2\) forces \(M\) to have at least \(\gamma(\delta)k^2\) edges (for \(\varepsilon \ll \delta\)). Moreover \(M\) is approximately \(K_5\)-free. Since \(M\) is a fixed graph (once \(\varepsilon\) and \(\delta\) are chosen), it possesses a triangle-free vertex set \(T \subset [k]\) of size at least \(c(\delta)k > 0\). Let \(S\) be the union of the clusters indexed by \(T\); then \(|S| \ge c(\delta)n\).

All edges inside clusters and all edges between non-adjacent pairs of \(M\) may be deleted while retaining \(\ge (\delta/2)n^2\) edges (for \(n\) large). Every triangle in the induced subgraph on \(S\) must therefore use at least one "bad" edge (an edge whose cluster pair is non-adjacent in \(M\)). There are \(O(\varepsilon n^2)\) bad edges. Each bad edge lies in at most \(O(n)\) triangles (at most \(k\) candidate clusters for the third vertex, each contributing \(O(n/k)\) common neighbors by regularity). Hence \(G[S]\) contains at most \(O(\varepsilon n^3)\) triangles. Selecting a random subset of \(S\) with retention probability \(p\) yields expected size \(\Theta(pn)\) and expected surviving triangles \(O(\varepsilon n^3 p^3)\). Forcing the latter below the former requires \(p = O(1/(n\sqrt{\varepsilon}))\), producing only an \(\Omega_\delta(1)\) vertex set, not \(\Omega(n)\).

An inductive approach on vertex neighborhoods encounters similar obstacles. Let \(v\) be a vertex of degree \(d \ge 2\delta n\). The induced subgraph \(H = G[N(v)]\) is \(K_4\)-free. If \(H\) itself satisfies the edge-density hypothesis (with a suitably smaller constant \(\delta'\)), a hypothetical inductive hypothesis on \(K_4\)-free graphs would supply a triangle-free induced subgraph of \(H\) on \(\gg d = \gg n\) vertices. Otherwise \(e(H)\) is negligible. Let \(U = V(G) \setminus (N(v) \cup \{v\})\), so \(|U| \ge (1-2\delta)n\). If the induced subgraph on \(U\) has many edges it again falls under the inductive hypothesis. Forcing both \(e(H)\) and \(e(U)\) small pushes the bulk of the edges into the cut \((N(v),U)\). While this bipartite graph is dense, constructing a large triangle-free subset of \(U\) (or of \(N(v) \cup U\)) that avoids all \(2U+1N\) triangles requires controlling codegrees across the cut; standard random sampling or greedy deletion again yields only sublinear sets when parameters are chosen to keep all triangle types under control.

Dependent-random-choice variants (selecting a small random "witness" set in \(U\) and retaining vertices of \(N(v)\) with few neighbors in the witness) similarly fail to produce a uniform linear lower bound independent of the hidden constants in the \(O(\cdot)\) bounds on triangles, because the worst-case codegrees permitted by \(K_4\)-freeness in neighborhoods still allow \(\Theta(n)\) common neighbors per edge.

Each attempted method either produces only \(o(n)\) vertices or relies on an inductive hypothesis that circles back to the original statement (or a closely related one for \(K_4\)-free graphs). The extremal example \(T(n,4)\) does contain a triangle-free induced subgraph on \(\sim n/2\) vertices (any two parts), but perturbing it while preserving \(K_5\)-freeness and edge density appears to allow constructions where every linear-size subset contains a triangle, at least within the limits of the above techniques. Consequently I could not solve the problem.
