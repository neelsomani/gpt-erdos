**Explanation**: The greedy Sidon sequence is defined by iteratively selecting the minimal integer that maintains the Sidon property (all pairwise sums \(a + b\) with \(a \leq b\) distinct). Let \(r(N) = |A \cap [1, N]|\) and let \(a_k\) denote the \(k\)-th term (so \(r(N) \asymp k\) when \(N \asymp a_k\)).

Any Sidon set in \([1, N]\) satisfies \(r(N) \ll N^{1/2}\), since the \(\binom{r(N)+1}{2}\) distinct sums lie in \([2, 2N]\). The question of whether the greedy choice attains \(r(N) \gg_\epsilon N^{1/2 - \epsilon}\) (equivalently, whether \(a_k = N^{o(1)} \cdot k^{2 + o(1)}\)) is therefore a question of how close to maximal the greedy sequence is.

The value \(m > M\) is forbidden if \(m + a_i \in S\) for some \(i \in [k]\) or \(2m \in S\). Thus the forbidden set in \((M, X]\) is contained in
\[
\bigcup_{i=1}^k \bigl( (S - a_i) \cap (M, X] \bigr) \cup \tfrac12 S.
\]
The total number of pairs \((s, a_i)\) is \(O(k^3)\), but this only immediately implies (via the pigeonhole principle on an interval of length \(\gg k^3\)) that \(a_{k+1} < 2M\) whenever \(M \gg k^3\), which yields the very weak conclusion \(r(N) \gg \log N\).

Improving this requires controlling the number of *distinct* elements of \((S - A) \cap (M, X]\). This set is contained in the difference set of two sumsets generated by \(A\), and its cardinality depends on both the additive energy of \(A\) and the distribution of \(S\) in \([1, 2M]\). Crude bounds on multiplicities (via the number of solutions to \(a' + a'' - a_i = m\)) produce only logarithmic or polylogarithmic savings, insufficient to reach an exponent \(1/2 - \epsilon\) in \(r(N)\). Assuming by induction a growth law such as \(a_j \asymp j^{2 + \delta}\) and attempting to derive a contradiction or bootstrap a uniform distribution of \(S\) likewise fails to close rigorously, as the error terms from clustering of sums near \(2M\) or repeated differences cannot be controlled without additional arithmetic information about \(A\).
