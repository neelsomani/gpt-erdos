Write
[
\alpha_k =\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_k(n)}{n!},\qquad \sigma_k(n)=\sum_{d\mid n} d^k .
]

This is **not fully settled for all (k\ge 1)**.

What is known:

* **(k=1) and (k=2):** (\alpha_k) is **irrational** (this is attributed to Erdős–Kac in later papers). ([sunsite3.icm.edu.pl][1])
* **(k=3):** (\alpha_3) is **irrational** (proved unconditionally). ([Springer][2])
* **(k=4):** (\alpha_4) is **irrational** (proved by Kyle Pratt, 2022). 

What is **not** known (unconditionally):

* For **(k\ge 5)**, the irrationality of (\alpha_k) is (as far as the standard references show) **still open**.

What is known **conditionally** (if you assume strong prime-conjectures):

* Schlage-Puchta proved that **Schinzel’s Hypothesis H implies** (\alpha_k) is irrational (in particular, for every $k$). ([Springer][2])
* Friedlander–Luca–Stoiciu proved irrationality for (k\ge 4) assuming a form of the **prime (k)-tuples conjecture**. ([sunsite3.icm.edu.pl][1])

So the best current “yes/no” answer is:

* **Yes** for (k=1,2,3,4).
* **Unknown** for general (k\ge 5) (but it would follow from standard prime-tuple type conjectures). ([Springer][2])
