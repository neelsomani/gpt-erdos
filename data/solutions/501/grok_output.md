**Problem 501**

We interpret the problem in graph-theoretic terms. Define an (undirected) graph \(G\) with vertex set \(\mathbb{R}\) by declaring an edge between distinct \(x, y \in \mathbb{R}\) precisely when \(x \in A_y\) or \(y \in A_x\). An independent set in \(G\) is then exactly a set \(X \subseteq \mathbb{R}\) satisfying the stated condition \(x \notin A_y\) for all distinct \(x, y \in X\). The first question asks whether \(G\) must admit an infinite independent set when each \(A_x\) is bounded with \(m^*(A_x) < 1\). The second asks whether \(\alpha(G) \geq 3\) when each \(A_x\) is additionally closed (hence Lebesgue measurable with \(m(A_x) < 1\)).

To decide the first question one must determine whether the family \(\{A_x\}_{x \in \mathbb{R}}\) can be chosen so that the complement graph \(\overline{G}\) is triangle-free yet still forces every independent set in \(G\) to be finite. The boundedness of each \(A_x\) implies that for any fixed \(x\) the set \(A_x\) can intersect only a bounded portion of \(\mathbb{R}\). The constraint \(m^*(A_x) < 1\) limits how much Lebesgue mass each \(A_x\) can place on any interval, but places no uniform bound on the locations or diameters of the \(A_x\). Consequently, points lying sufficiently far apart relative to the diameters of their respective \(A\)-sets automatically form independent pairs, yet the diameters may be made to grow with \(|x|\) (while keeping measure less than 1 by concentrating mass on thin Cantor-like sets of measure \(< 1\)). This flexibility suggests that a carefully chosen transfinite construction, perhaps employing the axiom of choice to enumerate points and assign thin but strategically placed forbidden sets, might keep all independent sets finite. No such construction has been exhibited, nor has a proof been found that every such family admits an infinite independent set (e.g., via repeated selection of points outside the outer-measure-\(< 1\) forbidden neighborhoods while controlling diameters).

For the second question, closedness of the \(A_x\) makes the relation \((x, y) \mapsto (x \in A_y)\) closed off the diagonal in \(\mathbb{R}^2\). One might hope to exploit compactness on large compact intervals or apply a topological selection argument to produce three mutually non-adjacent vertices. Suppose for contradiction that \(\alpha(G) \leq 2\). Then for every triple of distinct points at least one pair is joined by an edge, i.e., at least one of the two closed sets \(A_x, A_y\) contains the other point. Because each \(A_x\) is compact of measure \(< 1\), its complement in any interval of length \(> 2\) has positive measure, suggesting room to pick a third point outside all relevant \(A\)-sets. However, the sets \(\{y : x \in A_y\}\) need not be measurable and can be dense or co-dense independently of the measure of the \(A_y\). Attempts to derive a contradiction by integrating the indicator functions of the \(A_x\) over a large interval, or by iterating the selection of a point \(x_1\), removing its closed “forbidden” neighborhood of measure \(< 1\), and repeating, fail to control the diameters simultaneously for all three points. Thus no proof that \(\alpha(G) \geq 3\) is obtained, and no explicit family of compact sets of measure \(< 1\) with \(\alpha(G) \leq 2\) has been constructed either.

After exhaustive examination of measure-theoretic, topological, and choice-based constructions for both parts, the questions remain unresolved.
