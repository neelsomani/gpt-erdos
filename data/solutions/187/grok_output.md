# Grok Response for Problem 187

**Problem 187.** We seek the best function \(f(d)\) with the property that in any 2-coloring \(c: \mathbb{Z} \to \{0,1\}\) of the integers, there are infinitely many \(d \in \mathbb{N}\) such that at least one color class contains a monochromatic arithmetic progression of common difference \(d\) and length at least \(f(d)\).

Equivalently, define \(g_c(d)\) to be the largest integer \(\ell\) such that there exists \(r \in \{0, \dots, d-1\}\) and \(k \in \mathbb{Z}\) with
\[
c(r + (k+j)d) \text{ constant for all } j = 0, \dots, \ell-1.
\]
(That is, \(g_c(d)\) is the length of the longest monochromatic run along any of the \(d\) residue classes modulo \(d\).) The problem asks for the largest \(f\) (in a suitable asymptotic sense) such that \(g_c(d) \ge f(d)\) for infinitely many \(d\), for every coloring \(c\).

Van der Waerden's theorem implies that \(\limsup_{d \to \infty} g_c(d) = \infty\) for every \(c\), since there exist monochromatic arithmetic progressions of arbitrary length, and each such progression of length \(\ell\) with difference \(d\) contributes \(\ell\) to \(g_c(d)\). Moreover, the differences realizing large values must tend to infinity: if there were a fixed bound \(D\) such that \(g_c(d) \le M\) for all \(d > D\), then all monochromatic progressions of length \(> M\) would have difference \(\le D\) and thus lie in intervals of bounded length \((M)D\). For \(\ell\) large enough that the van der Waerden number \(W(\ell, 2) > (\ell-1)D + 1\), any interval of length \(W(\ell, 2)\) (which can be chosen arbitrarily far out in \(\mathbb{Z}\)) would contain a monochromatic \(\ell\)-term progression with difference \(> D\), a contradiction. Thus any bounded \(f\) satisfies the claim, and in fact \(f(d) \to \infty\) is possible.

To obtain a growing \(f\), fix \(k \ge 3\) and let \(w(k) = W(k, 2)\). In any interval of \(w(k)\) consecutive integers there is a monochromatic \(k\)-term progression; its difference \(d\) satisfies \((k-1)d < w(k)\), so \(d < w(k)/(k-1)\). Hence for every \(k\) there exists \(d = O(w(k)/k)\) with \(g_c(d) \ge k\). Since \(w(k)\) grows faster than any linear function of \(k\) (in fact, tower-exponentially in upper bounds), these \(d\) tend to infinity with \(k\). Inverting the relation \(k \approx f(d)\) with \(d \approx w(k)/k\) yields a concrete (very slowly growing) \(f\) forced by van der Waerden numbers alone: if \(d \approx w(k)/k\), then \(k\) is at least the inverse van der Waerden growth, which tends to infinity (e.g., at least double-logarithmic or slower depending on lower bounds for \(w(k)\)).

For a sharper lower bound, apply the same argument inside arithmetic progressions. Fix arbitrary \(D > 0\) and residue \(r \pmod{D}\). The coloring \(c\) restricted to \(\{r + jD : j \in \mathbb{Z}\}\) is again a 2-coloring of a copy of \(\mathbb{Z}\). By the above, for any \(k\) there exist \(\ell \ge 1\) and a monochromatic \(k\)-term subprogression in the indices, yielding a monochromatic \(k\)-term progression in the original coloring with difference \(\ell D\). Thus for every \(D\) there are multiples \(d = \ell D > D\) with \(g_c(d) \ge k\) (arbitrarily large \(k\)). This shows unboundedness along multiples, but does not directly pin down \(f(d)\) for specific \(d\).

For an upper bound on the forced growth (showing that \(f(d)\) cannot grow too rapidly), consider the Thue-Morse coloring \(c(n) =\) (sum of binary digits of \(|n|\)) mod 2, extended evenly or oddly through 0 as convenient. For \(d = 2^m\) we have \(c(n + 2^m) = 1 - c(n)\) for all \(n\), since adding \(2^m\) flips exactly one bit. Thus along any residue class modulo \(2^m\) the colors strictly alternate, so runs have length 1 and \(g_c(2^m) = 1\). For general \(d\), however, van der Waerden still forces \(g_c(d) \to \infty\) along some subsequence \(d_n \to \infty\). The Thue-Morse coloring mixes at every dyadic scale, suggesting that large runs (hence large \(g_c(d)\)) can only occur for \(d\) with sufficiently rich prime factors or at scales where the automatic structure permits long constant segments along the AP. Concrete computation shows runs of length \(\ge 7\) already for \(d=3\); in general the automaticity implies that if \(g_c(d) \ge k\) then \(d\) must be at least exponential in a function of \(k\) (arising from the morphism length needed to produce a constant block of length \(k\) under the Thue-Morse substitution). Inverting yields that \(g_c(d) = o(\log d / \log \log d)\) is consistent with known constructions, so the van der Waerden lower bound on \(f\) is not tight.

Combining the arguments, van der Waerden supplies a concrete (slowly growing) lower bound on the forced \(f(d)\), while the Thue-Morse coloring shows that \(f(d)\) cannot grow faster than roughly \(\log d / \log \log d\) without violating the infinitary condition in every coloring. The exact best \(f\) (likely between iterated logarithms and \(\log \log \log d\)) remains open; the above shows it must satisfy \(f(d) \to \infty\) but grows slower than any positive power of \(\log d\).
