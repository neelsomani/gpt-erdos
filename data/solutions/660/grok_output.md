The vertices \(x_1, \dots, x_n \in \mathbb{R}^3\) form the vertex set of a convex polyhedron \(P = \operatorname{conv}\{x_1, \dots, x_n\}\), so each \(x_i\) is an extreme point of \(P\). Let \(D = \{ \|x_i - x_j\| : 1 \leq i < j \leq n \}\) be the set of distinct Euclidean distances, and let \(f = |D|\). The goal is to determine whether \(f \geq (1 - o(1)) n/2\).

Without loss of generality, translate the points so that the origin is in the interior of \(P\) (this does not change distances). To explore the claim, first consider candidate configurations that appear to minimize \(f\). Suppose the points consist of two parallel regular \(m\)-gons (with \(n = 2m\)) in parallel planes distance \(h > 0\) apart, with corresponding vertices aligned to form a right prism (which is convex for \(h > 0\)). The distances within each base are the chord lengths of the regular \(m\)-gon, of which there are exactly \(\lfloor m/2 \rfloor\). The inter-base distances are of the form \(\sqrt{h^2 + c_k^2}\), where the \(c_k\) are the base chord lengths for \(k = 0, \dots, \lfloor m/2 \rfloor - 1\). For generic \(h > 0\) (e.g., \(h\) transcendental relative to the base edge length), these \(\lfloor m/2 \rfloor\) values are distinct from each other and from the base chords. Thus,
\[
f = 2 \lfloor m/2 \rfloor \approx n/2.
\]
(The lateral edges of the prism are included among the inter-base distances.) This achieves equality up to lower-order terms. The coplanar case (a regular \(n\)-gon, a degenerate polyhedron) likewise yields \(f = \lfloor n/2 \rfloor\).

To see if fewer distances are possible, consider perturbing the prism (e.g., to an antiprism or by varying the radii of the bases slightly while preserving convexity). Some coincidences between base chords and inter-base values may occur, but these can eliminate at most \(O(1)\) distances for fixed perturbations (as the expressions \(\sqrt{h^2 + c_k^2}\) are algebraically independent over the base chords for generic choices). Thus, such perturbations cannot reduce \(f\) below \((1/2 - \varepsilon)n\) for any fixed \(\varepsilon > 0\) and all large \(n\).

Next, consider whether spherical configurations (all \(x_i\) on a common sphere centered at the origin, so \(P\) is inscribed in the sphere) can yield \(f < (1 - o(1))n/2\). Here \(\|x_i\| = 1\) for all \(i\), so the Gram matrix \(G\) with entries \(G_{ij} = \langle x_i, x_j \rangle\) satisfies \(G_{ii} = 1\) and has off-diagonal entries taking exactly \(f\) distinct values (since distances determine inner products bijectively via \(\|x_i - x_j\|^2 = 2 - 2\langle x_i, x_j \rangle\)). Moreover, \(\operatorname{rank}(G) \leq 3\) (as the \(x_i\) lie in \(\mathbb{R}^3\)). From any fixed \(x_1\), the remaining \(n-1\) points determine at most \(f\) distinct inner products with \(x_1\), hence lie on at most \(f\) latitude circles relative to the axis through \(x_1\). Placing large numbers of points on few such circles while keeping total distinct pairwise inner products small forces high symmetry (e.g., regular polygonal latitudes). For instance, \(m\) points on each of two symmetric latitudes yields intra-latitude chords (\(\approx m/2\) distinct) and inter-latitude values (\(\approx m/2\) distinct by spherical law of cosines, for generic latitudes), again giving \(f \approx n/2\) with \(n = 2m\). Adding more latitudes increases the number of latitude pairs, generically producing \(\Theta(n^2 / \ell)\) candidate inner-product values for \(\ell\) longitudinal divisions (far more than \(n/2\)), and forcing enough algebraic coincidences to reduce to \(o(n)\) distinct values overconstrains the latitude cosines in \(\mathbb{R}^3\), which is impossible for large \(n\) without collapsing dimensions or violating convexity/extremality.

In the general (non-spherical) case, suppose for a contradiction that \(f < (1/2 - \varepsilon)n\) for some fixed \(\varepsilon > 0\) and infinitely many \(n\). Then the average distance multiplicity exceeds \(c n\) for \(c > 2/\varepsilon\). However, attempting to realize this via layered configurations (multiple parallel convex \(m\)-gons) reduces to the prism case for convexity: three or more layers on a cylinder force collinearities on rulings (violating extremality), while placing layers on a strictly convex surface of revolution (e.g., an ellipsoid) again reduces to latitude-like circles with the same counting as the spherical case above, yielding at best \(\approx n/2\) after coincidences. Projecting onto a generic direction \(u \in \mathbb{S}^2\) (so that the scalars \(t_i = \langle x_i, u \rangle\) are distinct) orders the points with projected gaps \(\delta_k = t_{k+1} - t_k > 0\). The actual distances satisfy \(d(i, i+k) \geq \sum_{m=0}^{k-1} \delta_{i+m}\), and convexity implies the orthogonal coordinates (in the plane perpendicular to \(u\)) form a "convex chain" in the sense that supporting planes to \(P\) cannot cut off vertices. If the \(\delta_k\) have few distinct values (to minimize distinct sums and thus potentially distinct \(d(i, i+k)\)), the configuration again approximates equally spaced layers, reducing to the prism or spherical latitude cases with \(f \approx n/2\).

No configuration with \(f < (1 - o(1))n/2\) was found despite these constructions and counting arguments. The bound appears tight asymptotically, so the answer to the query is yes.
