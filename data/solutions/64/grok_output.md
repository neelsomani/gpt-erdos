# Grok Response for Problem 64

**I couldn't solve it.**

I attempted a proof by assuming a minimal counterexample \(G\) (finite, \(\delta(G) \geq 3\), no cycle of length \(2^k\) for \(k \geq 2\)) and deriving a contradiction. First, \(G\) must contain cycles (as it is not a tree). If all cycles were odd, each vertex on a shortest odd cycle \(C\) (which must be induced) has degree at least 3, so at least one neighbor off \(C\). Finiteness then forces additional edges or paths to satisfy the degree condition everywhere, but any such connection between distinct vertices of \(C\) (or between pending structures attached to \(C\)) produces an even cycle: a chord spanning an even (respectively odd) arc of the odd cycle \(C\) yields an odd (respectively even) smaller cycle, and attaching trees to raise degrees inevitably requires closing edges whose fundamental cycles have lengths whose parities force an even cycle. Thus \(G\) contains even cycles; the shortest even cycle has length at least 6 (no \(C_4\)).

Let \(C\) be a shortest even cycle, of length \(\ell \geq 6\) and not a power of 2. Because \(\delta(G) \geq 3\), there exist chords or paths off \(C\) that create additional even cycles. Symmetric differences in the cycle space over \(\mathrm{GF}(2)\) yield new even subgraphs whose lengths are of the form \(\ell_1 + \ell_2 - 2k\) for integers \(k \geq 0\) arising from shared edges. Controlling the possible values of \(k\) (via shortest-path distances or ear decompositions) appeared promising for reaching a length exactly \(2^m\), but the absence of \(C_4\) and \(C_8\) restricts allowable \(k\) in ways that resisted exhaustive case analysis on the overlap parity and girth.

Constructions of candidate counterexamples (C4-free cubic graphs, bipartite cages of girth at least 6 such as the Heawood graph on 14 vertices, or larger (3,\(g\))-cages with \(g > 8\)) were examined. Each either contained a \(2^k\)-cycle (verifiable by enumeration on \(\leq 30\) vertices or by counting closed walks of even length in the incidence graph of a projective plane) or else introduced longer cycles whose lengths, when combined via symmetric difference, again produced a forbidden length before the order of the graph was reached. Induction on \(|V(G)|\) likewise failed: removing a vertex of degree 3 and adding edges among its neighbors either created a smaller counterexample (contradicting minimality) or immediately produced a \(C_4\) or \(C_8\).

No contradiction was reached, and no explicit counterexample on a small number of vertices could be exhibited. Thus the problem remains unresolved by the approaches tried.
