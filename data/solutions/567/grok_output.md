**Problem 567.** Let \(G\) be one of \(Q_3\), \(K_{3,3}\), or \(H_5\) (where \(H_5\) is the graph on five vertices obtained from \(C_5\) by adding two vertex-disjoint chords). Write \(R(G,H)\) for the Ramsey number: the smallest integer \(n\) such that every graph \(F\) on \(n\) vertices contains a copy of \(G\) as a (not necessarily induced) subgraph or the complement \(\overline{F}\) contains a copy of \(H\). The question is whether
\[
R(G,H)\ll m
\]
holds whenever \(H\) is a graph with \(m\) edges and no isolated vertex (the implied limit being as \(m\to\infty\) through all such \(H\)).

To decide the claim one must determine the growth, relative to \(m=e(H)\), of the largest order \(n\) admitting a graph \(F\) on \(n\) vertices that is \(G\)-free while \(\overline{F}\) is \(H\)-free. Equivalently, one seeks the supremum of all \(n\) such that there exists an edge-coloring of \(K_n\) with no monochromatic red \(G\) and no monochromatic blue \(H\).

**Case \(G=K_{3,3}\).** Suppose first that \(H\) is a matching of size \(m\). Let \(A\) be a set of \(2m-1\) vertices and \(B\) a set of \(n-(2m-1)\) vertices. Define \(F\) to have no edges inside \(A\), all edges inside \(B\), and all possible edges between \(A\) and \(B\). Then \(\overline{F}\) consists of a clique on \(A\) together with \(|B|\) isolated vertices, so the matching number of \(\overline{F}\) is at most \(m-1\). Thus \(\overline{F}\) is \(H\)-free. However \(F\) itself contains \(K_{3,3}\) as soon as \(|A|\ge 3\) and \(|B|\ge 3\): the complete bipartite graph between any three vertices of \(A\) and any three vertices of \(B\) supplies all nine required cross edges (extra edges inside \(B\) are irrelevant for subgraph containment). Consequently this particular \(H\) does not force a superlinear lower bound on \(R(K_{3,3},H)\). Any construction that tries to keep the matching number of \(\overline{F}\) small while rendering \(F\) \(K_{3,3}\)-free must therefore avoid large complete bipartite subgraphs between large independent sets. By the Kővári–Sós–Turán theorem the maximum number of edges in a \(K_{3,3}\)-free graph on \(n\) vertices is \(O(n^{5/3})\), but here we control the complement instead of the edge count; the constraint propagates to a \(O(m^{5/3})\) upper bound on \(n\) only after additional extremal arguments that have not been made rigorous for arbitrary \(H\).

**Case \(G=Q_3\).** The 3-cube is bipartite and 3-regular on eight vertices. Any attempted construction analogous to the one above (large clique plus independent set with all cross edges) immediately contains \(Q_3\) once both parts are large enough, because the required 12 edges of the cube can be selected from the complete bipartite section plus a matching inside the clique side. Forbidding all such embedded cubes while keeping \(\overline{F}\) free of a given \(H\) with \(m\) edges again appears to limit the order \(n\) to \(o(m)\), but converting the geometric constraints of the hypercube into a concrete extremal bound on \(n\) in terms of \(m\) requires a uniformity lemma or supersaturation result that is unavailable.

**Case \(G=H_5\).** Here \(G\) contains an odd cycle, so both \(F\) and \(\overline{F}\) must avoid triangles and longer odd cycles in certain configurations. If \(H\) is again a matching, Turán-type graphs (e.g., balanced complete bipartite graphs) can be used on the complement side to bound the matching number, but then \(F\) itself becomes a union of two cliques and instantly contains \(H_5\) (the chords of \(H_5\) sit inside one clique). Perturbing the construction to destroy all copies of \(H_5\) while preserving the matching bound on the complement forces the part sizes to shrink with \(m\), suggesting once more that \(n=o(m)\). The concrete constants and asymptotic remain elusive.

In all three cases the special structure of \(G\) (bipartiteness with forbidden complete bipartite subgraph, or odd girth with chords) prevents the standard extremal examples that produce \(R(G,H)=\Omega(m)\) for general \(G\). Known general theorems (e.g., the Chvátal–Rödl–Szemerédi–Trotter bound linear in \(v(H)\), or results of Burr–Erdős–Spencer on sparse \(H\)) give only \(R(G,H)=O(m)\) or worse; sharpening to \(o(m)\) seems to demand a finer control on the interaction between \(G\)-freeness and the edge-distribution constraints imposed by an arbitrary \(H\)-free complement. After examining matching examples, complete-bipartite constructions, Turán graphs, and random perturbations, no counter-example sequence \(H_m\) with \(R(G,H_m)\ge c\,m\) could be constructed, nor could a uniform \(o(m)\) upper bound be proved.
