# Grok Response for Problem 741

**Problem 741**

We seek to determine two related questions in additive combinatorics. Let \(A \subseteq \mathbb{N}\). Write \(A + A = \{a + b : a, b \in A\}\) for the sumset (with the understanding that \(a = b\) is allowed). A set \(S \subseteq \mathbb{N}\) is said to have *positive density* if its lower asymptotic density
\[
\underline{d}(S) = \liminf_{N \to \infty} \frac{|S \cap [1, N]|}{N} > 0.
\]
A set \(S \subseteq \mathbb{N}\) has *bounded gaps* (equivalently, is syndetic) if there exists \(K \in \mathbb{N}\) such that every interval of \(K\) consecutive integers intersects \(S\); equivalently, the gaps between consecutive elements of \(S\) are bounded by a constant independent of position.

The first question asks whether \(A + A\) having positive density forces the existence of a partition \(A = A_1 \sqcup A_2\) (disjoint union) such that both \(A_1 + A_1\) and \(A_2 + A_2\) have positive density. The second asks whether there exists an additive basis \(A\) of order \(2\) (i.e., there exists \(N_0\) such that every integer \(n \geq N_0\) lies in \(A + A\)) with the property that *no* such partition makes both \(A_1 + A_1\) and \(A_2 + A_2\) syndetic.

To approach the first question, suppose \(\underline{d}(A + A) = \delta > 0\). One might hope to construct the desired partition by a random 2-coloring of \(A\): assign each element of \(A\) independently to \(A_1\) or \(A_2\) with equal probability. For a fixed sum \(s \in A + A\), the probability that \(s \notin A_1 + A_1\) depends on the number \(r(s)\) of representations \(s = a + b\) (\(a \leq b\)). If representations are unique and disjoint across distinct \(s\), the events "\(s \in A_1 + A_1\)" and "\(s \in A_2 + A_2\)" are independent Bernoulli random variables with success probability \(1/4\) each. The expected density of each monochromatic sumset would then be at least \(\delta/4 > 0\). A second-moment argument or Lovász Local Lemma might then show that both monochromatic sumsets have positive density with positive probability, provided the representation graph has bounded degree (so that dependence is local).

However, when representations overlap or when \(A\) has additive structure, the events become dependent in a manner difficult to control uniformly. To test robustness, consider a clustered construction. Let \((N_k)_{k \geq 1}\) be rapidly increasing and let \(I_k = [N_k, N_k + L_k]\) with \(L_k \to \infty\) (chosen so that the sum intervals \(I_k + I_k = [2N_k, 2N_k + 2L_k]\) are disjoint and their union has lower density \(\delta > 0\)). Set \(A = \bigcup_k I_k\). Then \(A + A = \bigcup_k (I_k + I_k)\). For any partition, split each \(I_k\) into \(B_{1,k} = I_k \cap A_1\) and \(B_{2,k} = I_k \cap A_2\) with \(|B_{i,k}| = m_{i,k}\). Any integer set of cardinality \(m\) has sumset of cardinality at most \(2m - 1\). Thus if both \(m_{1,k}, m_{2,k} \geq \varepsilon L_k\), each monochromatic sumset covers at least a positive proportion (linear in \(\varepsilon\)) of the block of length \(\approx 2L_k\). Summing over blocks recovers positive lower density for both monochromatic sumsets, since the original blocks already contribute density \(\delta\). Splitting each \(I_k\) evenly (e.g., into its "lower half" and "upper half") realizes this: the lower-half sums occupy an interval of length \(\approx L_k\) at the bottom of \(I_k + I_k\), and the upper-half sums occupy a comparable interval at the top, each contributing density \(\approx \delta/2 > 0\).

This suggests the statement may hold when \(A\) decomposes into sufficiently independent clusters. The difficulty arises when cross-summands between clusters fill a positive-density portion of \(A + A\) while individual clusters have small doubling. In such cases, assigning elements to \(A_1\) versus \(A_2\) can inadvertently suppress one monochromatic sumset below positive density by destroying cross terms without sufficiently enriching the monochromatic terms. Controlling the asymptotic lower density while balancing both colors appears to require a uniform ergodic or density-increment argument that has not been found. Greedy assignment (at each stage adding the next element of \(A\) to the color whose current sumset gains the most new elements up to the current horizon) likewise fails to guarantee positive *lower* density, as early choices can force later large gaps that drive the liminf to zero.

For the second question, the requirement is stronger: both monochromatic sumsets must be syndetic. Suppose \(A\) is a thin basis of order 2 (e.g., \(|A \cap [1, n]| \asymp (\log n)^C\) for large \(C\)) with essentially unique representations for most large integers. Each large \(n\) is essentially \(a + b\) for a unique pair \(\{a, b\} \subseteq A\). Coloring \(a\) and \(b\) differently excludes \(n\) from both monochromatic sumsets. If one can arrange the unique representations so that, in every 2-coloring of \(A\), there exist arbitrarily long intervals in which every integer has a cross-colored representation, then at least one color class will miss that entire interval, yielding unbounded gaps. Constructing such an \(A\) explicitly requires careful placement of "representation bridges" across potential long intervals while preserving that \(A\) remains a basis. Attempts to build \(A\) inductively—ensuring at stage \(k\) that a prospective long gap interval \(J_k\) (of length growing with \(k\)) can only be covered by pairs that straddle both colors in every prior partial coloring—lead to conflicts with the basis property: forcing too many cross-color constraints eventually leaves some later integer without any representation at all. No such basis has been rigorously constructed, nor has a proof been found that every basis of order 2 admits a partition into two sets with syndetic sumsets (e.g., via taking \(A_1 = A \cap 2\mathbb{N}\) and \(A_2 = A \cap (2\mathbb{N}+1)\) and verifying syndeticity from the basis property, which fails for lacunary bases).

After examining random-coloring arguments, clustered constructions with controlled doubling, greedy procedures, unique-representation bases, and inductive constructions aimed at forcing large monochromatic gaps, no resolution of either statement has been obtained.
