This is **open**.

It is Erdős–Graham problem #455, and as far as the current literature records, nobody knows whether every prime sequence with **nondecreasing gaps**
[
q_{n+1}-q_n\ge q_n-q_{n-1}
]
must satisfy
[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{q_n}{n^2}=+\infty.
]
([Erdős Problems][1])

What *is* known is that such a sequence cannot grow *subquadratically*: Richter proved the quantitative lower bound
[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{q_n}{n^2}>0.352\ldots,
]
so in particular (q_n) is (\gg n^2) (with an absolute constant). ([Erdős Problems][1])

But whether the ratio (\frac{q_n}{n^2}) must actually **tend to infinity** (rather than staying bounded along some cleverly chosen convex prime subsequence) remains unknown. ([Erdős Problems][1])
