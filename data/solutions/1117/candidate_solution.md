Write (M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|) and
[
\nu(r)=|\\{z:\ |z|=r,\ |f(z)|=M(r)\\}|.
]
For non‑monomial entire $f$, (\nu(r)) is finite for each (r>0). 

### (\displaystyle \limsup_{r\to\infty}\nu(r)=\infty)?

Yes.

This is exactly part $a$ of a question posed by Erdős. Herzog and Piranian (1968) constructed a transcendental entire $f$ for which
[
\nu(n)=n\quad\text{for every integer }n\in\mathbb N,
]
so in particular (\limsup_{r\to\infty}\nu(r)=\infty). 

[[nomath]](Their construction does not give simple control of $\nu(r)$ for *non-integer* $r$, which is relevant for the next part.)[[/nomath]] 

### (\displaystyle \liminf_{r\to\infty}\nu(r)=\infty)?

As of the most recent sources I can find (up through the 2024 journal version of the arXiv preprint below), this remains **open**.

Glücksam and Pardo‑Simón explicitly state Erdős’ part $b$ as asking whether (\nu(r)\to\infty) [[nomath]](equivalently $\liminf_{r\to\infty}\nu(r)=\infty$)[[/nomath]], and they note that this “has eluded an answer to this date.” 
Marchenko’s 2022 survey paper likewise presents $b$ as an open “Clunie/Erdős” problem. 

What *is* known in that direction: Glücksam–Pardo‑Simón construct an entire function with **arbitrarily many “almost maximum” arcs** on every sufficiently large circle [[nomath]](for any fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the number of connected components of ${|z|=r,\ |f(z)|>M(r)-\varepsilon}$ tends to $\infty$)[[/nomath]]. But they emphasize that their approximation method does not currently let them conclude that actual maximum points themselves must tend to (\infty). 

So:

* **(\limsup_{r\to\infty}\nu(r)=\infty): yes** (Herzog–Piranian).
* **(\liminf_{r\to\infty}\nu(r)=\infty): unknown/open** (as of these references).
