# Grok Response for Problem 124

**Problem 124**

We interpret \(P(d,k)\) as the additive basis consisting of all finite sums of distinct terms \(d^i\) for \(i\geq k\) (including the empty sum \(0\)). Thus each element of \(P(d,k)\) has a base-\(d\) representation using only the digits \(0\) and \(1\), starting from the \(d^k\) place onward. The first question asks whether the condition
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r\frac{1}{d_i-1}\geq 1
\]
(with \(3\leq d_1<d_2<\dots<d_r\)) is sufficient to guarantee that every sufficiently large integer \(N\) admits a representation
\[
N=\sum_{i=1}^r c_ia_i,\qquad c_i\in\{0,1\},\quad a_i\in P(d_i,0).
\]
(The coefficients \(c_i\) merely allow omission of a summand.) The second question adds the coprimality hypothesis \(\gcd(d_1,\dots,d_r)=1\) and asks the analogous statement for \(P(d_i,k)\) with arbitrary but fixed \(k\geq 1\).

To approach the first question we compare growth rates. Let \(A_d(X)=\#\{a\in P(d,0):a\leq X\}\). The largest power of \(d\) not exceeding \(X\) is \(\lfloor\log_d X\rfloor\), so there are \(\Theta(\log_d X)\) admissible powers and
\[
A_d(X)\asymp X^{\log_d 2}.
\]
(The implied constant depends only on \(d\).) Write \(\alpha_d=\log_d 2\in(0,1/2]\) for \(d\geq 3\). The number of sums \(\sum_{i=1}^r a_i\) with each \(a_i\in P(d_i,0)\) and total sum at most \(X\) is then at most
\[
\prod_{i=1}^r A_{d_i}(X)\asymp X^{\sum\alpha_{d_i}}.
\]
If \(\sum\alpha_{d_i}>1\) the exponent exceeds \(1\), suggesting that the sumset may be dense enough to cover all large integers. However the given hypothesis is \(\sum 1/(d_i-1)\geq 1\), which is numerically unrelated to \(\sum\log_{d_i}2\). For concrete triples the two quantities can point in opposite directions: e.g., \(d_1=3\), \(d_2=4\), \(d_3=7\) yields
\[
\frac12+\frac13+\frac16=1,\qquad \log_32+\log_42+\log_72\approx 0.6309+0.5+0.3562=1.487>1,
\]
while \(d_1=3\), \(d_2=5\), \(d_3=6\) yields
\[
\frac12+\frac14+\frac15=0.95<1,\qquad \log_32+\log_52+\log_62\approx 0.6309+0.4307+0.3869=1.448>1.
\]
Thus the supplied linear condition on reciprocals does not coincide with the natural dimensional threshold arising from the counting functions. A direct volume argument therefore gives no conclusive information.

A second approach proceeds by generating functions. The indicator function of \(P(d,0)\) has generating function
\[
\prod_{j=0}^\infty(1+x^{d^j}).
\]
The sumset in question corresponds to the product of \(r\) such generating functions (one per \(d_i\)). To show that all sufficiently large coefficients are positive one would like to prove that the product
\[
\prod_{i=1}^r\prod_{j=0}^\infty(1+x^{d_i^j})
\]
has no gaps beyond a certain point. Because the bases \(d_i\) are distinct and at least \(3\), the exponents \(d_i^j\) are multiplicatively independent over \(\mathbb{Q}\) in generic cases. Standard lacunary-series techniques (e.g., Cantor-like constructions or Fourier analysis on the circle) then imply that the support of the product measure has Hausdorff dimension \(\sum\log_{d_i}2\). When this sum exceeds \(1\) the support is dense in \(\mathbb{R}^+\) in the sense of Lebesgue measure, but transferring density to the integers requires effective discrepancy estimates or explicit covering lemmas that are unavailable for mixed bases. When some \(d_i\) share a common factor the situation is even worse: all summands may lie in a fixed residue class modulo that factor, immediately producing infinite arithmetic progressions of non-representable integers.

For the second question the coprimality assumption \(\gcd(d_1,\dots,d_r)=1\) removes the modular obstruction. Moreover, requiring each summand to lie in \(P(d_i,k)\) for large \(k\) means discarding finitely many small powers; the tail still has the same asymptotic density \(X^{\alpha_{d_i}}\). Thus the dimensional heuristic is unchanged. Nevertheless, the absence of small “adjustment” terms makes it harder to fill small gaps. One can attempt an inductive construction: suppose all integers up to \(X\) are representable using powers at least \(d_i^k\). When \(X\) exceeds the least common multiple of the moduli generated by the highest powers so far, the coprimality lets one hope to solve a simultaneous congruence lifting problem. The lifting step reduces to showing that the difference set
\[
\bigl(P(d_1,k)-P(d_1,k)\bigr)+\dots+\bigl(P(d_r,k)-P(d_r,k)\bigr)
\]
contains a complete set of residues modulo \(M\) for a suitable \(M\) growing with \(k\). Because each difference set \(P(d_i,k)-P(d_i,k)\) is a lacunary sumset with gaps of size \(\approx d_i^k\), the sum of \(r\) such sets has gaps whose size is governed by the largest \(d_i^k\). Controlling these gaps uniformly in \(k\) demands quantitative versions of the Hardy–Littlewood circle method or bilinear Fourier restriction estimates that have not been established for mixed lacunary bases.

After examining both the counting-function obstructions, the mismatch between the given linear condition and the natural dimensional threshold \(\sum\log_{d_i}2\), the modular obstructions when the \(d_i\) are not coprime, and the lifting difficulties for large \(k\), no proof or counter-example could be obtained for either claim. Therefore the questions remain open.
