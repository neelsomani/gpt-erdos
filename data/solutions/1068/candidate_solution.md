With the standard meaning of **“infinitely connected” = infinitely *vertex*-connected** (i.e. for any two vertices there are infinitely many pairwise vertex‑disjoint paths between them), this is **currently open**.

* This question is explicitly listed as an open problem (Erdős Problems #1068), phrased as: does every graph with (\chi(G)=\aleph_1) contain a **countable** infinitely vertex‑connected subgraph? ([Erdős Problems][1])
* Bowler–Pitz also single it out as an **open remaining version** of the Erdős–Hajnal problem. ([arXiv][2])

What *is* known around it:

* You **cannot** in general demand an **uncountable** infinitely connected subgraph [[nomath]](let alone one of chromatic number $\aleph_1$)[[/nomath]]: Soukup constructed, in ZFC, an uncountably chromatic graph where **every uncountable vertex set** contains two vertices connected by only **finitely** many independent (vertex‑disjoint) paths; Bowler–Pitz give a simpler such construction. ([Erdős Problems][1])
  So any guaranteed “infinitely connected” subgraph—if it always exists—might have to be **countable**, which is exactly why your version is interesting.

A common source of confusion is **edge‑ vs vertex‑connectivity**:

* If instead “infinitely connected” is interpreted as **infinite edge‑connectivity**, then there *is* a positive theorem: Thomassen proved that every graph of **uncountable chromatic number** has a subgraph of **uncountable chromatic number** and **infinite edge‑connectivity**. ([Welcome to DTU Research Database][3])
  But this does **not** settle the vertex‑connectivity version asked above (and the open problem #1068 is specifically about vertex‑connectivity). ([Erdős Problems][1])

So, for your question as usually understood in this area: **we don’t currently know whether the answer is “yes” or “no.”** ([Erdős Problems][1])
