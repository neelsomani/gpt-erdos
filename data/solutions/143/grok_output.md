# Grok Response for Problem 143

**The Condition and Its Implications**

Let \(A = \{x_1 < x_2 < \cdots \}\) with \(x_i > 1\) and \(x_{i+1} \geq x_i + 1\) (which follows from the \(k=1\) case). The condition \(|kx - y| \geq 1\) for all distinct \(x, y \in A\) and integers \(k \geq 1\) implies that, for any fixed \(x \in A\), the set of admissible locations for any new \(y\) excludes intervals of length 2 centered at each point \(kx\) (\(k \geq 1\)).

To analyze sparsity, partition \((1, \infty)\) into dyadic intervals \(I_m = [2^m, 2^{m+1})\) for \(m \geq 0\). Let
\[
H_m = \sum_{x \in A \cap I_m} \frac{1}{x}, \qquad S_M = \sum_{m=0}^M H_m
\]
be the harmonic increment in \(I_m\) and the partial harmonic sum up to \(2^{M+1}\), respectively. Then
\[
\sum_{x \in A} \frac{1}{x \log x} \asymp \sum_{m \geq 1} \frac{H_m}{m},
\]
since \(\log x \asymp m\) uniformly on \(I_m\) (up to constants). The partial sum \(\sum_{x < n,\, x \in A} 1/x = S_M + o(1)\) for \(n \approx 2^{M+1}\). Thus, both target statements hold if \(S_M = O(1)\), while divergence of \(\sum H_m/m\) requires \(S_M\) to grow at least on the order of \(\log \log 2^M\) along a subsequence (by comparison with the integral test or partial summation).

**Forbidden Measure in Later Intervals**

Fix \(M > m\) large and consider admissible \(y \in I_M\). For each prior \(x \in A \cap I_m\) (\(m < M\)), the relevant multiples \(kx\) with \(kx \in I_M\) correspond to \(k \approx 2^{M-m}\). There are \(\Theta(2^{M-m})\) such \(k\), and each contributes a forbidden interval of length 2. These intervals are spaced \(x \asymp 2^m\) apart. Since \(x > 2\) eventually (elements \(\leq 2\) block all but a discrete set of measure zero, incompatible with infinitely many points separated by \(\geq 1\)), there is no overlap among the forbidden intervals generated by a single \(x\). The total forbidden length generated by this \(x\) in \(I_M\) is thus \(\Theta(2^{M-m+1})\), for a proportion \(\Theta(2/x) = \Theta(2^{1-m})\) of \(|I_M| = 2^M\).

Summing over all prior \(x < 2^M\), the total forbidden measure in \(I_M\) is at most
\[
\min\bigl(2^M, \, C \cdot S_{M-1} \cdot 2^M\bigr)
\]
for an absolute constant \(C > 0\) (accounting for the factor of 2 in each interval length). Equivalently, the forbidden *proportion* is at most \(\min(1, C \cdot S_{M-1})\). When overlaps occur across different prior \(x\) (inevitable if \(S_{M-1} \gtrsim 1\)), the actual covered proportion is strictly smaller, but still \(\geq c \cdot \min(1, S_{M-1})\) on average if the centers \(kx\) are incommensurate.

The admissible measure in \(I_M\) is therefore at most \(2^M \cdot \max(0, 1 - C \cdot S_{M-1})\) in the non-overlapping regime. Since points of \(A\) must be separated by \(\geq 1\), the maximal cardinality \(N_M = |A \cap I_M|\) satisfies
\[
N_M \leq 2^M \cdot \max(0, 1 - C \cdot S_{M-1}) + o(2^M).
\]
Hence the increment obeys
\[
H_M \leq \max(0, 1 - C \cdot S_{M-1}) + o(1).
\]
If \(S_{M-1} > 1/C\), the increment is \(o(1)\). Iterating, once \(S_M\) exceeds \(1/C\), subsequent increments are forced toward zero (up to the \(o(1)\) from boundary effects and possible residual gaps from overlaps). This bounds \(S_M = O(1)\) uniformly in \(M\), since each increment is \(\leq 1\) and large increments are possible only while \(S_{M-1}\) remains bounded away from \(1/C\).

**Convergence of the Sum**

With \(S_M = O(1)\), we have \(\sum_m H_m < \infty\). Therefore
\[
\sum_m \frac{H_m}{m} < \infty,
\]
since \(H_m \geq 0\) and the partial sums of \(H_m\) are bounded (by the comparison test or Kronecker's lemma). This yields
\[
\sum_{x \in A} \frac{1}{x \log x} < \infty.
\]
The stronger statement \(\sum_{x < n,\, x \in A} 1/x = O(1) = o(\log n)\) follows immediately.

**Remarks on Sharpness and Overlaps**

The bound relies on the linear growth of forbidden measure with \(S_{M-1}\). Residual gaps from heavy overlaps (when \(S_{M-1} \gg 1\)) cannot systematically permit \(H_M \asymp 1\) infinitely often, as the centers \(kx \bmod 1\) for distinct prior \(x\) are dense in \([0,1)\) for generic choices; aligning them to force macroscopic gaps in *every* subsequent \(I_M\) would violate the separation \(|x_i - x_j| \geq 1\) in prior intervals. Thus \(S_M\) cannot grow unbounded while preserving admissible room for large \(H_M\).

A construction with \(S_M \to \infty\) (e.g., \(x_n = 3^n + 1/2\)) yields only \(S_M = O(1)\) anyway. Attempts to add clusters of width \(L_n \asymp 3^n / n^2\) around such centers produce forbidden zones of width \(\Theta(k L_n)\) at level \(k \approx 3^{m-n}\), swallowing prospective later clusters and forcing \(H_m = o(1)\) eventually.

Hence the condition forces \(A\) to be sparse in both senses.

**Conclusion**

The condition implies both
\[
\sum_{x \in A} \frac{1}{x \log x} < \infty
\]
and
\[
\sum_{\substack{x < n \\ x \in A}} \frac{1}{x} = o(\log n).
\]
