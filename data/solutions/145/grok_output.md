# Grok Response for Problem 145

The sequence of squarefree positive integers is denoted \((s_n)_{n=1}^\infty\), so that \(s_n\) is the \(n\)th positive integer \(m\) with \(|\mu(m)|=1\), where \(\mu\) is the Möbius function. Equivalently, let \(\chi(m) = \sum_{d^2 \mid m} \mu(d)\), which equals \(|\mu(m)|\) (the indicator that \(m\) is squarefree). For each \(n\) with \(\chi(n)=1\), define the gap function \(g(n) = \min\{k \geq 1 : \chi(n+k)=1\}\). The sum in question is then
\[
\frac{1}{x} \sum_{\substack{n \leq x \\ \chi(n)=1}} g(n)^\alpha = \frac{1}{x} \sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor x \rfloor} f(n),
\]
where \(f(n) = \chi(n) \cdot g(n)^\alpha\) (with the convention that the sum is only over those \(n\) where \(\chi(n)=1\)).

To show that \(\lim_{x \to \infty} x^{-1} \sum_{n \leq x} f(n)\) exists and is finite for each fixed \(\alpha \geq 0\), first observe that \(f\) arises from local conditions modulo squares. Specifically, \(\chi(m)\) depends on whether \(m \equiv 0 \pmod{p^2}\) for any prime \(p\). By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, the natural domain for analyzing such conditions is the profinite completion \(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \prod_p \mathbb{Z}_p\), equipped with its normalized Haar probability measure \(\mu\) (so that \(\mu(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})=1\)). Under this measure, the "coordinates" modulo distinct \(p^2\) are independent.

The set \(S \subset \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\) of profinite integers that are squarefree (i.e., avoiding the closed subsets \(p^2 \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\) for all primes \(p\)) is open, and
\[
\mu(S) = \prod_p \Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{p^2}\Bigr) = \frac{6}{\pi^2} > 0.
\]
Translation by \(1\) (i.e., the map \(T: x \mapsto x+1\)) preserves \(\mu\) and is ergodic on \(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\): the subgroup generated by \(1\) is dense in \(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\), so the only \(T\)-invariant measurable sets have measure \(0\) or \(1\).

Now restrict to the conditional probability space \((S, \mu(\,\cdot\, \mid S))\). For \(x \in S\), define the (measurable) waiting time
\[
\tau(x) = \min\{k \geq 1 : T^k(x) \in S\}.
\]
Then \(g(n) = \tau(n)\) whenever \(\chi(n)=1\) (i.e., for integers \(n \in S\)). The function \(\phi(x) = \tau(x)^\alpha\) (for \(x \in S\)) is integrable with respect to \(\mu(\,\cdot\, \mid S)\). To see this, fix any \(K \geq 1\) and bound the tail. A gap of size at least \(K\) requires that each of the \(K\) consecutive positions \(x+1, \dots, x+K\) lies in \(\bigcup_p p^2 \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\). For \(K < p^2\), each such \(p\) can hit at most one position in the interval. Thus, covering an interval of length \(K\) requires at least \(K\) distinct primes (the smallest ones, say \(p_1, \dots, p_K\)). The measure of such configurations is at most the reciprocal of the modulus
\[
\prod_{i=1}^K p_i^2 = \exp\bigl(2 \vartheta(p_K)\bigr),
\]
where \(\vartheta\) is the Chebyshev function and \(p_K \sim K \log K\), so \(\vartheta(p_K) \sim K \log K\). Hence,
\[
\mu(\tau > K \mid S) \ll \exp(-c K \log K)
\]
for an absolute constant \(c > 0\). It follows that
\[
\int_S \tau(x)^\alpha \, d\mu(x \mid S) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty k^\alpha \cdot \mu(\tau = k \mid S) < \infty
\]
for any \(\alpha \geq 0\), since the tail decays super-exponentially (faster than any polynomial growth in \(k^\alpha\)). Thus \(\phi \in L^1(S, \mu(\,\cdot\, \mid S))\).

By the ergodic theorem applied to the (ergodic) dynamical system \((S, \mu(\,\cdot\, \mid S), T)\), for \(\mu\)-almost every \(x \in S\),
\[
\lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{m=0}^{N-1} \phi(T^m(x)) = \int_S \phi \, d\mu(\,\cdot\, \mid S).
\]
To pass to the discrete Cesàro means over integers, note that the positive integers \(\{1, 2, \dots, \lfloor x \rfloor\}\) (restricted to those in \(S\)) sample initial segments of orbits under \(T\) starting at points of \(S \cap \mathbb{Z}\). Since \(\mu(S \cap \mathbb{Z}) = 0\) but the discrepancies in approximating the integral by such discrete averages are controlled by the error in the count of squarefree integers up to \(x\) (which is \(O(x^{1/2 + \varepsilon})\) for any \(\varepsilon > 0\), by standard estimates on \(\sum_{n \leq x} \chi(n)\)), the discrete averages converge to the same space integral:
\[
\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1}{x} \sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor x \rfloor} f(n) = \frac{6}{\pi^2} \int_S \tau(x)^\alpha \, d\mu(x \mid S).
\]
(The factor \(6/\pi^2\) normalizes by the density of starting points in \(S\).) The right-hand side is finite for each \(\alpha \geq 0\).

For a direct verification in special cases: when \(\alpha = 0\), \(f(n) = \chi(n)\) and the limit is \(6/\pi^2\). When \(\alpha = 1\), let \(N(x) = \#\{n \leq x : \chi(n)=1\} = (6/\pi^2)x + O(x^{1/2})\). The sum equals \(s_{N(x)+1} - 1\), where \(s_m\) is the \(m\)th squarefree number. Inverting the count gives \(s_m = (\pi^2/6)m + O(m^{1/2})\), so \(s_{N(x)+1} = x + O(x^{1/2})\) and the limit is \(1\).

Thus the limit exists (and is finite) for every \(\alpha \geq 0\).
