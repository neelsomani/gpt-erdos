# Grok Response for Problem 761

**On the first question.** Let \(G\) be an undirected graph. Recall that an orientation \(D\) of \(G\) is acyclic if it contains no directed cycle. For a digraph \(D\), write \(\vec{\chi}(D)\) for the minimum number of colours needed to colour \(V(D)\) so that each colour class induces an acyclic subdigraph of \(D\). The dichromatic number is then
\[
\delta(G) := \min\{k : \text{for every orientation \(D\) of \(G\), we have \(\vec{\chi}(D) \le k\}}\,.
\]
A proper \(k\)-colouring of \(G\) yields an acyclic colouring in every orientation (each colour class is edgeless), so \(\delta(G) \le \chi(G)\). The converse inequality fails already for small values: every odd cycle \(C_{2m+1}\) satisfies \(\chi(C_{2m+1}) = 3\). Orienting all edges consistently around the cycle produces a directed cycle. Any proper subset of its vertices induces a directed path (or a disjoint union of paths), which is acyclic. Thus any 2-colouring has both colour classes acyclic, whence \(\delta(C_{2m+1}) = 2\).

To decide whether \(\chi(G) \to \infty\) forces \(\delta(G) \to \infty\), it is therefore necessary to decide whether there exists a constant \(K\) such that \(\delta(G) \le K\) for a sequence of graphs with chromatic numbers tending to infinity. Subgraph-monotonicity supplies one obstruction: if \(H \subseteq G\) and \(D_H\) is any orientation of \(H\), extend the orientation arbitrarily to an orientation \(D\) of \(G\). Any acyclic colouring of \(D\) restricts to an acyclic colouring of \(D_H\), so \(\delta(H) \le \delta(G)\). In particular \(\delta(K_n) \to \infty\) (a random tournament on \(n\) vertices has largest transitive subtournament of order \(O(\log n)\) with high probability, forcing at least \(\Omega(n/\log n)\) colours). Hence any graph with bounded dichromatic number is \(K_n\)-free for all large \(n\).

Triangle-free graphs of arbitrarily large chromatic number exist (Mycielski, shift graphs, random graphs of appropriate density). For any such \(G\), the clique obstruction vanishes. Nevertheless, producing an explicit orientation that forces \(\vec{\chi}(D) > 2\) appears difficult. Suppose an orientation \(D\) is chosen so that every set \(S \subseteq V(G)\) with \(|S| \ge |V(G)|/2\) contains a directed cycle. Then no 2-colouring can be acyclic. Constructing such a \(D\) inside a high-chromatic triangle-free graph while preserving the global chromatic number seems possible in principle (high girth makes short cycles easy to orient cyclically, and sufficiently many overlapping odd cycles might block all balanced bipartitions), but no concrete example is known and no proof that the construction must fail has been found either.

**On the second question.** The cochromatic number \(\zeta(G)\) is the smallest \(k\) such that \(V(G)\) admits a partition into \(k\) sets, each inducing a clique or an independent set. Large \(\zeta(G)\) forces both \(G\) and \(\overline{G}\) to have large chromatic number in a weak sense: at least one of \(\chi(G)\) or \(\chi(\overline{G})\) is at least \(\sqrt{\zeta(G)}\). The question asks whether a graph with large \(\zeta\) must contain (as a subgraph or induced subgraph) a subgraph \(H\) with large \(\delta(H)\).

Again subgraph-monotonicity of \(\delta\) reduces the problem to whether high cochromatic number forces a large clique (already answered negatively by triangle-free graphs with large cochromatic number) or some other subgraph with large dichromatic number. Mycielski graphs have cochromatic number tending to infinity while remaining triangle-free; the same construction supplies no obvious subgraph with dichromatic number larger than 2. Random orientations of these graphs do not manifestly require more than two acyclic sets, but proving that every orientation admits a 2-colouring with both parts acyclic would require a global structural theorem about acyclic partitions that is not available. Conversely, exhibiting a single orientation that defeats all candidate 2-partitions inside every high-cochromatic graph has also resisted proof.

After examining the monotonicity properties, the behaviour on cliques and odd cycles, the existence of high-chromatic triangle-free graphs, and several attempted constructions (Mycielski graphs, random orientations, overlapping directed odd cycles), no proof that either implication holds or fails could be obtained. Therefore the questions remain open.
