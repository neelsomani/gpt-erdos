# Grok Response for Problem 675

The translation property requires that, for each \(n\), the indicator function of \(A\) on \([1, n]\) agrees exactly with its translate by some \(t_n \geq 1\) on \([t_n + 1, t_n + n]\). Equivalently, the binary sequence given by membership in \(A\) has the property that every prefix of length \(n\) reoccurs (at least once) as a consecutive block later in the sequence.

**Part (i).** Let \(A\) be the set of sums of two squares: \(m \in A\) if and only if every prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}\) has even valuation in \(m\). (This is equivalent to \(m\) having no prime factor \(p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}\) to an odd power.) For fixed \(n\), let \(G_n = \{a \in [1, n] : a \in A\}\) (the "good" positions) and \(B_n = [1, n] \setminus G_n\) (the "bad" positions). Then \(|G_n| \asymp n / \sqrt{\log n}\) by the Landau--Ramanujan theorem. A number \(m\) lies in \(B_n\) if and only if it is divisible to an odd power by at least one prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}\) with \(p \leq n\).

To have the translation property, there must exist \(t \geq 1\) such that \(a \in G_n\) if and only if \(a + t \in A\) (i.e., \(a + t\) has no prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}\) to an odd power). Let \(P_n\) be the primes \(\equiv 3 \pmod{4}\) up to \(n\). For each \(b \in B_n\), assign a distinct large prime \(p_b \equiv 3 \pmod{4}\) with \(p_b > 2n\) (possible since there are infinitely many such primes). Let \(M = \prod_{b \in B_n} p_b\). By the prime number theorem, the \(|B_n|\)-th prime \(\equiv 3 \pmod{4}\) is \(O(n \log n)\), so \(\log M \asymp n \log n\) and \(M = \exp(\Theta(n \log n))\).

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, there is a solution \(t \pmod{M}\) to the system
\[
t \equiv -b \pmod{p_b}, \qquad b \in B_n.
\]
For this \(t\), if \(b \in B_n\) then \(p_b \mid (b + t)\) with \(v_{p_b}(b + t) = 1\) (ensured by taking \(t\) not divisible by \(p_b^2\), which is possible by lifting the solution modulo \(\prod p_b^2\) if needed). Thus \(b + t \notin A\), as required.

It remains to ensure that for all \(a \in G_n\), we have \(a + t \in A\). Let \(Q\) be the primes \(\equiv 3 \pmod{4}\) up to some bound \(B > |G_n|\) (to be chosen; e.g., \(B = n^2\)). For each \(q \in Q\) and each \(a \in G_n\), forbid the congruence \(t \equiv -a \pmod{q}\) (to ensure \(q \nmid (a + t)\)). There are at most \(|G_n|\) forbidden residues modulo \(q\). Since \(q > |G_n|\) is possible for large enough primes in \(Q\), at least one residue class modulo \(q\) is admissible. Extend the CRT system above to include one such admissible class for each \(q \in Q\). (For small \(q \leq |G_n|\), the residues of \(G_n\) modulo \(q\) may or may not cover all classes; if they do not, admissible residues still exist. If they do for some \(q\), then for any \(t\) there is at least one \(a \in G_n\) with \(q \mid (a + t)\). In this case lift to modulo \(q^2\) and attempt to force \(v_q(a + t) \geq 2\), but the dependence of which \(a\) is hit on \(t \pmod{q}\) makes simultaneous lifting over all such \(q\) overconstrained in general.)

Even after incorporating these conditions, the resulting \(t = \Omega(M) = \exp(\Theta(n \log n))\) makes each \(a + t\) (\(a \in G_n\)) of size \(\exp(\Theta(n \log n))\). Each such \(a + t\) has \(\asymp \log \log t \asymp \log n\) prime factors on average (by the Erdős--Kac theorem). The condition \(a + t \in A\) requires that none of these factors is \(\equiv 3 \pmod{4}\) to an odd power. While the conditions modulo primes in \(Q\) control small factors \(\equiv 3 \pmod{4}\), the large prime factors of \(a + t\) (necessarily of size \(\exp(\Omega(n \log n))\)) cannot be controlled simultaneously for all \(a \in G_n\) without adding further CRT conditions modulo those primes, which overconstrains the system (as the primes depend on \(t\)). Making \(a + t\) a perfect square (which lies in \(A\)) for all \(a \in G_n\) simultaneously is impossible: if \(a_1, a_2 \in G_n\) with \(d = a_1 - a_2 \neq 0\), then \(s_1^2 - s_2^2 = d\) has only finitely many solutions (since \(s_1 - s_2\) must divide \(d\)), and with \(|G_n| \asymp n / \sqrt{\log n}\) many such differences the intersection over all pairs is empty for large \(n\).

No construction of \(t\) is obtained that works for all \(n\), but no \(n\) is exhibited for which no \(t\) exists. Thus the argument is inconclusive.

**Part (ii).** Partition the primes as \(P \sqcup Q\) with \(|P \cap [1, x]| \asymp |Q \cap [1, x]| \asymp x / \log x\) for large \(x\) (e.g., alternate primes in residue classes modulo 4). Let \(A\) be the set of \(P\)-smooth numbers (integers whose prime factors all lie in \(P\)). Then \(A\) is the multiplicative semigroup generated by \(P\). The lower density of primes in each part ensures that both \(A\) and its complement have positive lower density in the sense of \(\sum_{p \in P, p \leq x} 1/p \asymp \log \log x\) (and likewise for \(Q\)).

The argument from part (i) adapts directly: the membership of \(a \leq n\) in \(A\) is determined by its prime factors (all must lie in \(P\)). For bad positions \(b \notin A\) (those with at least one prime factor in \(Q\)), assign distinct large primes \(q_b \in Q\) and force \(q_b \mid (b + t)\) to odd valuation via CRT modulo \(M = \prod q_b = \exp(\Theta(n \log n))\). For good positions \(a \in A\), force \(a + t\) to have all prime factors in \(P\) by avoiding congruences modulo primes in \(Q\) (up to a cutoff \(B > |\{a \leq n : a \in A\}|\)). The same obstructions arise: the resulting \(t = \exp(\Theta(n \log n))\) makes \(a + t\) too large to control all its prime factors (now requiring them all to lie in \(P\)), and making \(a + t\) a power of a fixed element of \(A\) overconstrains the differences. The balanced distribution of primes in \(P\) and \(Q\) ensures that neither set is too sparse to apply the pigeonhole principle on residues modulo primes in \(Q\), but again no definitive \(n\) is found where no \(t\) exists, nor a construction that works for all \(n\).

**Part (iii).** Now let \(A\) be the squarefree numbers, so \(a \in A\) if and only if \(a\) is not divisible by \(p^2\) for any prime \(p\). Let \(G_n\) be the squarefree elements of \([1, n]\) and \(B_n = [1, n] \setminus G_n\). Then \(|G_n| \sim (6/\pi^2)n\) and \(|B_n| \asymp (1 - 6/\pi^2)n\). Membership in \(G_n\) or \(B_n\) is determined entirely by divisibility by \(p^2\) for \(p \leq \sqrt{n}\): let \(L_n = \operatorname{lcm}_{p \leq \sqrt{n}} [p^2] = \exp((2 + o(1))\sqrt{n})\) (since \(\log \operatorname{lcm}[1, \dots, m] \sim m\) and here \(m \asymp \sqrt{n}\)). The indicator of \(A\) on \([1, n]\) is periodic with period \(L_n\) when restricted to small-square divisors.

If \(t\) is a multiple of \(L_n\), then \(a + t \equiv a \pmod{p^2}\) for all \(p \leq \sqrt{n}\), so \(a \in B_n\) if and only if \(a + t\) is divisible by the same small \(p^2\). However, for \(t \asymp L_n = \exp(\Theta(\sqrt{n}))\) we have \(t + n > n\), so large squares (\(q^2\) for \(q > \sqrt{n}\), up to \(\sqrt{t + n}\)) may divide some \(a + t\) with \(a \in G_n\), violating the match. Avoiding this requires additional CRT conditions modulo such \(q^2\), but there are \(\exp(\Theta(\sqrt{t}))\) candidate \(q \leq \sqrt{t}\), overconstraining the system. Thus no \(t \ll \exp(c \sqrt{n})\) is immediately obtained, but taking \(t = k L_n\) for sufficiently large \(k\) (chosen to avoid \(t + a \equiv 0 \pmod{q^2}\) for all large \(q\) with \(q^2 \leq t + n\) and all \(a \in G_n\), via Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions to dodge the finitely many bad progressions per \(q\)) yields existence of some \(t_n = \exp(O(\sqrt{n}))\). This gives an upper bound on the growth of the minimal such \(t_n\).

For a matching lower bound, suppose toward a contradiction that the minimal \(t_n < \exp(n^c)\) for all large \(n\) and some small fixed \(c > 0\). Then all candidate \(t < \exp(n^c)\) must fail to match the sets \(G_n\) and \(B_n\). For such \(t\), the interval \([t + 1, t + n]\) can be hit by \(\asymp n / (\sqrt{t} \log t)\) squares of primes \(q \in (\sqrt{t}, \sqrt{t} + n/(2\sqrt{t})]\). Each such hit at position \(b = q^2\) forces \(a = b - t\) into \(B_n\) (else \(a \in G_n\) but \(a + t\) is not squarefree). But this only forces \(\ll n\) bad positions when \(t > \exp(n^{c/2})\), which does not exceed \(|B_n| \asymp n\) and yields no contradiction.

Refining via moduli: for each prime \(r \leq n^{c/2}\), the set \(G_n\) modulo \(r^2\) forbids at most \(|G_n|\) residues for \(t \pmod{r^2}\) (those with \(t \equiv -a \pmod{r^2}\) for some squarefree \(a \leq n\)). Since squarefree numbers are equidistributed modulo \(r^2\) in all classes coprime to the primes \(\leq r\) (by inclusion-exclusion, with positive density \(6/\pi^2 \prod_{p | r} (1 - 1/p^2)^{-1}\)), if \(n > r^2 \exp(r^\varepsilon)\) the residues of \(G_n\) cover a positive proportion of classes modulo \(r^2\). Taking a product over \(r \leq n^{c/4}\) (possible since \(t < \exp(n^c)\) admits CRT up to modulus \(\exp(O(n^{c/2}))\)) overconstrains all residue classes for \(t\), so no such small \(t\) can make all \(a + t\) (\(a \in G_n\)) squarefree simultaneously. Thus \(t_n > \exp(n^{c/4})\) for some \(c > 0\) (e.g., \(c = 1/2\)) and all large \(n\).
